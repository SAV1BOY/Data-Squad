# Attribution Caveat Phrases

## Purpose

Standardized phrases for communicating the limitations and nuances of attribution. Attribution is one of the most commonly misunderstood areas of data. These phrases help set accurate expectations and prevent overconfidence in attribution claims.

## General Attribution Caveats

- "Attribution in this analysis uses a [first-touch / last-touch / multi-touch / linear] model. Results would differ under a different model."
- "We can attribute [X]% of conversions to a specific source with reasonable confidence. The remaining [Y]% involved multiple touchpoints."
- "This attribution reflects correlation, not proven causation. Users who [action] also tend to [outcome], but we have not isolated the causal mechanism."
- "The attribution window is [X] days. Interactions outside this window are not credited."
- "Self-reported attribution (e.g., 'How did you hear about us?') and data-driven attribution tell different stories. Both have value; neither is complete."

## Channel and Campaign Attribution

- "This channel is credited under our [model] attribution model. Under a [different model], credit would shift to [other channel]."
- "Organic and paid channels have overlapping influence. The true incremental contribution of paid is likely [lower/different] than the attributed number suggests."
- "Brand awareness campaigns influence conversions that get attributed to other channels. Direct attribution undervalues their contribution."
- "Cross-device behavior limits our attribution accuracy: [X]% of users interact on multiple devices, and we can only track [Y]% of cross-device journeys."
- "The attributed cost per acquisition for this campaign is $[X]. This does not account for [assisted conversions / brand halo effects / organic lift]."

## Experiment vs. Attribution

- "Unlike attribution, this experiment provides causal evidence because we controlled for [variables] using [randomization method]."
- "Attribution data suggested [channel] was the top driver. The controlled experiment revealed the actual incremental lift was [X]%, significantly lower."
- "We recommend running an incrementality test before scaling spend on this channel, as attributed performance may overstate true impact."
- "Holdout test results show that [X]% of attributed conversions would have occurred without the intervention."

## Multi-Touch Complexity

- "This conversion involved [X] touchpoints over [Y] days. Assigning credit to a single source oversimplifies the customer journey."
- "Under linear attribution, each of the [X] touchpoints receives equal credit. This may overvalue low-impact interactions."
- "Time-decay attribution gives more credit to touchpoints closer to conversion. In this case, [channel] receives [X]% of credit."
- "The customer journey for this segment involves an average of [X] touchpoints. Single-touch attribution misses [Y]% of the picture."

## Setting Expectations

- "No attribution model is perfectly accurate. We use [model] because it best fits our [business model / data availability / use case]."
- "Attribution should inform directional decisions, not precise budget allocation. Treat these numbers as approximations, not exact measurements."
- "We revisit our attribution methodology [quarterly/annually] to ensure it reflects current customer behavior."
- "When comparing attribution data across periods, note that the model was updated on [date]. Pre- and post-change numbers are not directly comparable."
- "Attribution accuracy degrades for longer sales cycles. For deals closing in [X]+ days, treat attribution data as directional only."

## Recommending Better Approaches

- "To improve attribution confidence for this question, we recommend running a [geo-holdout / time-based holdout / randomized controlled test]."
- "For high-stakes budget decisions, we recommend supplementing attribution data with [incrementality testing / media mix modeling]."
- "Combining attribution data with customer surveys provides a more complete picture than either source alone."
