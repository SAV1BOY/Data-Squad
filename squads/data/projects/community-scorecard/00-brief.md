# Community Scorecard - Brief

## Phase Objective

Define the community's purpose, the metrics needed to assess its health, and how community performance connects to broader business outcomes. This brief ensures that community measurement goes beyond vanity metrics (member count, post count) to capture the dimensions that actually indicate a thriving community: contribution quality, member engagement depth, and business value creation.

## Prerequisites

- Active community platform (forum, Slack, Discord, Circle, or similar)
- Community manager or team responsible for community strategy
- Understanding of why the community exists and what business goals it supports
- Access to community platform data (posts, reactions, members, activity logs)
- Stakeholder alignment on the community's strategic role in the business

## Steps

1. **Articulate the Community's Purpose**: Document the community's reason for existence in clear, specific terms. Communities serve different strategic functions: customer support deflection (reducing support ticket volume), product feedback and ideation (surfacing feature requests and use cases), customer retention (building switching costs through relationships), brand advocacy (generating word-of-mouth and referrals), or ecosystem development (enabling partnerships and integrations). The purpose determines which metrics matter most.

2. **Map Community Value to Business Outcomes**: For each stated purpose, define how community activity translates to measurable business impact. If the community exists for support deflection, map it to support ticket reduction and cost savings. If it exists for retention, map it to churn rate differences between community members and non-members. If it exists for advocacy, map it to referral rates and social proof generation. These connections justify continued investment in the community.

3. **Identify Stakeholders and Their Questions**: Document who needs to understand community health and what questions they need answered. The community manager needs daily operational metrics (activity levels, response times, flagged content). Marketing needs engagement and advocacy metrics. Product needs feature feedback and sentiment trends. Executives need ROI and strategic impact metrics. Each stakeholder's questions inform which scorecard components are needed.

4. **Audit Current Community Measurement**: Review what community metrics are currently tracked, how they are collected, and who uses them. Identify gaps between what is measured and what should be measured based on the community's purpose. Common gaps include measuring volume without quality (counting posts but not answer quality), measuring participation without depth (counting active members but not engagement intensity), and measuring activity without outcomes (counting interactions but not business results).

5. **Define the Community Health Dimensions**: Establish the 4-6 dimensions that collectively represent community health. A comprehensive framework includes Growth (is the community attracting new members at a sustainable rate), Engagement (are members actively participating beyond just viewing), Contribution (is valuable content being created and knowledge accumulating), Belonging (do members feel connected and identify with the community), and Business Impact (is the community delivering measurable business value).

6. **Assess Data Availability**: For each health dimension, inventory the data needed to measure it and determine whether that data is accessible. Community platform APIs may provide activity data. Product analytics may provide usage data for community members versus non-members. Support systems may provide ticket deflection data. Survey instruments may be needed for belonging and satisfaction metrics that are not captured through behavioral data.

7. **Set Measurement Cadence**: Determine how frequently each dimension needs to be measured. Operational metrics (daily active members, post volume, response time) should update daily. Engagement and contribution metrics should update weekly. Belonging metrics from surveys should update monthly or quarterly. Business impact metrics should update monthly or quarterly depending on the outcome measured.

8. **Define Success Targets**: For each dimension, set specific targets that define what a healthy community looks like. Targets should be based on benchmarks (industry standards for communities of similar size and type), historical performance (improvement over the community's own baseline), and business requirements (the level of community performance needed to deliver the stated business value).

## Deliverables

- Community purpose statement linking the community to specific business functions
- Business value map connecting community activities to measurable business outcomes
- Stakeholder registry with questions and metric needs per audience
- Current measurement audit identifying gaps and redundancies
- Community health dimension framework with 4-6 defined dimensions
- Data availability assessment for each dimension with identified gaps
- Measurement cadence schedule for each metric category
- Success targets for each dimension with benchmark sources and rationale

## Quality Gate

The brief is complete when all of the following conditions are met:

- The community's purpose is documented and agreed upon by the community team and business leadership
- At least two specific business outcomes are mapped to community activities with measurable connections
- All key stakeholders have been identified and their metric needs documented
- The health dimension framework covers both operational metrics and strategic impact
- Data availability has been assessed and gaps are identified with plans to close them
- Success targets are set with justification from benchmarks or business requirements
- Measurement cadence is defined and feasible given available data and survey infrastructure
- The community manager has reviewed and approved the measurement framework as comprehensive and practical
- A project timeline with milestones is established and accepted by all involved parties
