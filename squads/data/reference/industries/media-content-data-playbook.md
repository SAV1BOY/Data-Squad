# Media & Content Data Playbook

## Overview

Media and content businesses live or die by audience attention. Whether the
model is advertising-supported, subscription-based, or hybrid, the core
analytical challenge is understanding what content drives engagement, what
engagement drives revenue, and how to sustain both over time. The data
landscape is rich (every click, scroll, and view is tracked) but noisy —
separating signal from noise requires disciplined metric design.

---

## Core Metrics

### Engagement Metrics

**Engagement Time:** Total time spent consuming content per user per period.
More meaningful than pageviews or sessions because it measures actual attention.
Define "engaged time" carefully — exclude idle/background tabs. Segment by
content type, device, and traffic source.

**Content Completion Rate:** Percentage of users who consume content to the end
(video watch-through, article read-through). For video, track the drop-off
curve: where do viewers leave? For articles, track scroll depth as a proxy.

**Return Frequency:** How often users come back. Daily, weekly, and monthly
active users, plus the DAU/MAU ratio as a stickiness indicator. For media,
a DAU/MAU of 25-40% is typical; above 50% indicates strong habit formation.

**Session Depth:** Number of content pieces consumed per session. Deeper
sessions indicate effective content discovery and recommendation systems.

### Retention Metrics

**Subscriber Retention:** Monthly retention of paying subscribers. In media,
first-month retention is critical — users who survive month 1 have
significantly higher LTV. Track by acquisition channel and entry content.

**Free-to-Paid Conversion Rate:** Percentage of free users who convert to
paid subscription. Analyze by content consumption patterns — which articles,
videos, or features drive the paywall conversion?

**Churn by Reason:** Categorize churn into price sensitivity, content
dissatisfaction, competitive alternatives, life changes, and involuntary
(payment failure). Each requires a different intervention.

### Revenue Metrics

**Ad Revenue Per User (ARPU-Ad):** For ad-supported models, total ad revenue
divided by active users. Decompose into CPM (cost per thousand impressions),
ad fill rate, and impressions per user. Optimize all three.

**Subscriber LTV:** Total revenue from a subscriber over their lifetime.
In media, LTV varies dramatically by acquisition source — users acquired
via promotional pricing have lower retention than organic subscribers.

**Revenue Per Content Piece:** Total revenue (ad + subscription attribution)
generated by each piece of content. Helps prioritize content investment.
Attribution is challenging — a great article may not directly generate
revenue but may drive subscriptions.

**Content ROI:** Production cost vs. revenue generated per content piece or
content type. Essential for content investment decisions, but hard to
measure due to indirect value (brand, SEO, subscriber acquisition).

## Key Analytical Patterns

### Content Performance Analysis

Evaluate content across multiple dimensions:
- Reach (unique viewers/readers)
- Engagement depth (completion rate, time spent)
- Virality (shares, referral traffic)
- Conversion impact (paywall hits, subscription starts)
- Revenue generation (direct ad revenue, attributed subscription revenue)

No single metric captures content value. Build a composite content score
weighted by business objectives.

### Recommendation System Evaluation

If the product uses algorithmic content recommendations:
- Click-through rate on recommendations vs. editorial picks
- Engagement depth on recommended content vs. organic discovery
- Diversity of consumption (are recommendations creating filter bubbles?)
- Long-term retention impact (do recommendation-heavy users retain better?)

### Subscriber Lifecycle Analysis

Map the subscriber journey:
1. **Acquisition:** What content or promotion drove the subscription?
2. **Onboarding (month 1):** Do they establish a consumption habit?
3. **Habit formation (months 2-3):** Are they returning regularly?
4. **Renewal decision (month 12/end of promo):** Do they retain at full price?
5. **Mature subscriber (year 2+):** What keeps them engaged long-term?

Identify the "habit threshold" — the minimum consumption level in month 1
that predicts long-term retention.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Optimizing for clicks over engagement.** Clickbait drives pageviews but
   destroys reader trust and long-term retention. Track engagement quality
   (time, completion) not just volume.
2. **Promotional pricing masking true economics.** A $1/month promo drives
   sign-ups but those subscribers churn at 60%+ when price normalizes.
   Report LTV inclusive of promotional periods.
3. **Ignoring cannibalization.** New content may not grow the audience —
   it may simply redistribute attention from existing content. Track total
   engagement alongside per-content metrics.
4. **Vanity social metrics.** Shares and likes on social platforms do not
   necessarily translate to on-platform engagement or revenue. Use UTM
   tracking to connect social signals to actual business outcomes.
5. **Content investment without measurement.** Producing premium content
   without tracking its performance is guessing, not strategy. Every
   content investment should have measurable success criteria.
