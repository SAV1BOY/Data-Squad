# North Star Metric Audit

## Purpose

Audit the North Star Metric (NSM) for alignment, clarity, and resistance to gaming. The NSM should capture the core value the product delivers to customers. When it moves, both the customer and the business benefit. This checklist ensures the NSM is well-chosen, well-understood, and well-governed.

---

## Checklist Items

### 1. A Single North Star Metric Is Defined
- **Pass:** One metric is clearly identified as the NSM. It is documented, and the team can state it from memory.
- **Fail:** There are multiple "North Stars" (which means none of them are), or the team cannot agree on what the NSM is.

### 2. The NSM Reflects Customer Value Delivered
- **Pass:** The metric measures value received by the customer (e.g., "weekly active projects completed," "meals delivered," "messages sent to matches"), not just business output.
- **Fail:** The NSM is a business metric (revenue, signups) that does not directly capture whether customers are getting value.

### 3. The NSM Correlates with Revenue Over Time
- **Pass:** Historical analysis shows that when the NSM increases, revenue follows. The correlation is documented and the lag is understood.
- **Fail:** The NSM moves independently of business outcomes, meaning optimizing it does not help the business.

### 4. Input Metrics (Drivers) Are Identified
- **Pass:** The NSM is decomposed into 3-5 input metrics that the team can directly influence (e.g., activation rate, frequency, breadth of usage). Each input has an owner.
- **Fail:** The NSM exists as a single number with no understanding of what drives it up or down.

### 5. Gaming Potential Is Assessed
- **Pass:** The team has asked, "How could someone artificially inflate this metric without creating real value?" and has documented the gaming risks and mitigations.
- **Fail:** Gaming potential has not been considered, leaving the metric vulnerable to Goodhart's Law.

### 6. Guardrail Metrics Exist
- **Pass:** At least 2-3 guardrail metrics are defined to ensure NSM growth does not come at the expense of quality, customer satisfaction, or long-term health (e.g., NPS, churn rate, support ticket volume).
- **Fail:** The NSM is pursued without guardrails, risking growth at the expense of sustainability.

### 7. The NSM Is Trackable in Near-Real-Time
- **Pass:** The NSM can be measured at least weekly (ideally daily) with a lag of no more than 24-48 hours, enabling rapid feedback on experiments and initiatives.
- **Fail:** The NSM can only be computed monthly or quarterly, making it useless for operational decision-making.

### 8. Teams Can Connect Their Work to the NSM
- **Pass:** Every product and growth team can articulate how their current projects are expected to move the NSM or one of its input metrics.
- **Fail:** Teams work on projects with no stated connection to the NSM, suggesting alignment is superficial.

### 9. The NSM Is Reviewed for Continued Relevance
- **Pass:** The NSM is reviewed at least annually (or when the business model changes significantly) to confirm it still captures the core value exchange.
- **Fail:** The NSM was set years ago and has not been re-evaluated despite business model evolution.

### 10. The NSM Is Visible and Celebrated
- **Pass:** The NSM is displayed on a team dashboard, referenced in all-hands meetings, and wins are celebrated when it moves meaningfully.
- **Fail:** The NSM is defined in a document but not present in daily team conversations.
