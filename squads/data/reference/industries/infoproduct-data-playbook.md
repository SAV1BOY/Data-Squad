# Infoproduct Data Playbook

## Overview

Infoproducts — online courses, coaching programs, membership communities,
digital downloads, and knowledge-based businesses — have unique data
characteristics. Margins are high (often 80%+), but customer expectations
are shaped by the creator's personal brand, making satisfaction and refund
dynamics different from traditional SaaS or e-commerce. The data challenge
is measuring not just transactions, but whether customers actually
consume and benefit from the content they purchased.

---

## Core Metrics

### Sales and Revenue Metrics

**Conversion Rate by Funnel Stage:** Track the full acquisition funnel:
- Lead magnet opt-in rate (landing page visitors to email subscribers)
- Nurture-to-sales page rate (email list to sales page visit)
- Sales page conversion rate (visitors to purchase)
- Upsell acceptance rate (at checkout or post-purchase)

Each stage has different levers. Optimize the weakest link, not the easiest.

**Average Order Value (AOV):** Base product price plus upsells, order bumps,
and payment plan surcharges. In infoproducts, AOV is highly controllable
through pricing strategy and offer structure.

**Revenue by Product Tier:** Track revenue distribution across product tiers
(e.g., ebook at $47, course at $497, coaching at $5,000). Understand which
tier drives most revenue and which has the best margin.

**Payment Plan Completion Rate:** Percentage of customers on payment plans who
complete all installments. Incomplete payment plans represent recognized
revenue that never materializes. This is often 70-85% — meaning 15-30% of
payment plan revenue is phantom revenue.

### Consumption and Outcome Metrics

**Completion Rate:** Percentage of buyers who complete the core content.
Infoproduct completion rates are notoriously low (often 5-15% for self-paced
courses). Track by:
- Content format (video vs. text vs. workbook)
- Product price point (higher-priced products often have higher completion)
- Delivery format (self-paced vs. cohort-based vs. live)

**Module-Level Engagement:** Consumption by module or section. Identifies
where students drop off and which content resonates most. The drop-off
pattern reveals content quality issues, pacing problems, or misaligned
expectations.

**Implementation Rate:** Percentage of students who take the key action
the product teaches (e.g., launching a business, completing a project,
applying a framework). This is the ultimate outcome metric but requires
self-reporting or external tracking.

### Customer Quality Metrics

**NPS (Net Promoter Score):** Survey at multiple points: post-purchase (day 1),
mid-course, and post-completion. The gap between post-purchase NPS (excitement)
and mid-course NPS (reality) reveals expectation management issues.

**Refund Rate:** Percentage of purchases refunded within the guarantee window.
Healthy range is 3-8%. Below 3% may indicate under-marketing (only highly
committed buyers purchase). Above 10% indicates expectation mismatch,
product quality issues, or targeting problems.

**Testimonial / Case Study Generation Rate:** Percentage of customers who
provide testimonials or agree to case studies. A leading indicator of
satisfaction and a critical marketing asset.

**LTV (Lifetime Value):** Total revenue per customer across all products over
the full relationship. In infoproducts, LTV is driven by:
- Initial product purchase
- Upsell to higher-tier products
- Recurring membership or community fees
- Repeat purchases of new products
- Referral value (word of mouth, affiliate)

### Upsell and Expansion Metrics

**Upsell Conversion Rate:** Percentage of buyers of product A who purchase
product B. Track by timing (immediate upsell vs. delayed) and by which
product pairs convert best.

**Ascension Rate:** Percentage of customers who move up the value ladder
(e.g., from course to coaching, from membership to mastermind). The key
growth lever for mature infoproduct businesses.

**Repeat Purchase Rate:** Percentage of customers who buy more than one
product. Segment by first-product type and by time between purchases.

## Key Analytical Patterns

### Refund Analysis

Refunds in infoproducts are not random. Analyze by:
- Days to refund (day 1 refunds = buyer's remorse; day 28 refunds = content
  issues)
- Consumption before refund (did they even open the course?)
- Traffic source (some channels attract less qualified buyers)
- Price point (higher-priced products may have different refund patterns)
- Guarantee length (longer guarantees may increase or decrease refunds)

### Launch vs. Evergreen Analysis

Many infoproduct businesses use both launch models (time-limited enrollment)
and evergreen funnels (always available). Compare:
- Conversion rate (launches typically convert higher due to urgency)
- Refund rate (launch buyers may have more buyer's remorse)
- Completion rate (cohort-based launches often have better completion)
- LTV (which acquisition model produces higher-value customers?)

### Content-Revenue Correlation

Identify which free content (blog posts, podcasts, YouTube videos, social
media) drives paid conversions. This requires:
- UTM tracking on all content
- Attribution modeling for multi-touch journeys
- Content scoring by conversion impact, not just traffic

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Counting payment plan revenue as collected.** A $997 product on 4
   payments of $297 is not $997 in revenue until all payments clear.
   Track collected revenue separately from committed revenue.
2. **Ignoring completion rates.** Low completion rates eventually catch up
   through refunds, negative reviews, and declining referrals. Invest in
   completion as a growth lever.
3. **Vanity list size.** An email list of 50,000 with 5% open rates is less
   valuable than 5,000 with 40% open rates. Track engaged subscribers, not
   total subscribers.
4. **Launch revenue spikes masking trends.** Quarterly launches create
   revenue spikes that make it hard to see underlying trends. Track rolling
   averages and per-launch performance separately.
5. **Over-attributing to the last email.** In infoproduct funnels, purchases
   are influenced by weeks of nurturing. Last-click attribution to the final
   sales email understates the value of earlier content.
