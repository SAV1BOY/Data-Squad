# Ellis Growth Lever Audit

## Purpose

Determine which growth lever -- acquisition, activation, retention, referral, or monetization -- has the highest impact potential right now. Ellis teaches that growth teams must focus on the lever with the biggest current gap, not spread effort evenly. This checklist audits whether the right lever is getting attention.

---

## Checklist Items

### 1. All Five Levers Are Measured
- **Pass:** Current performance for each lever is quantified: acquisition volume and CAC, activation rate, retention curve, referral coefficient, and revenue per user. All five have recent data.
- **Fail:** Only 2-3 levers are measured, making it impossible to compare opportunities across the full set.

### 2. Each Lever's Current State Is Benchmarked
- **Pass:** Each lever's performance is compared to industry benchmarks, historical internal performance, or cohort-based targets. The gap between current and achievable is estimated.
- **Fail:** Lever performance is known in absolute terms but not compared to any reference, so the team cannot tell which lever is underperforming.

### 3. The Highest-Leverage Lever Is Identified
- **Pass:** A clear, data-backed argument identifies which lever offers the most impact per unit of effort. This is documented and agreed upon by the team.
- **Fail:** The team works on multiple levers simultaneously with no explicit prioritization, or the choice is driven by personal preference rather than data.

### 4. Sensitivity Analysis Is Performed
- **Pass:** A simple model shows the business impact of a 10% improvement in each lever, making it clear which lever moves the needle most (usually retention or activation for established products).
- **Fail:** The team selects levers intuitively without modeling the relative impact.

### 5. The Focus Lever Has Dedicated Resources
- **Pass:** The majority (60%+) of growth team effort is directed at the identified highest-leverage lever. Other levers get maintenance effort or are handled by other teams.
- **Fail:** Effort is spread evenly across all levers, or the identified lever gets the same allocation as lower-priority ones.

### 6. Lever Selection Is Reviewed Quarterly
- **Pass:** Every quarter, the lever analysis is refreshed because improvements in one lever shift the bottleneck to another. The team consciously rotates focus as needed.
- **Fail:** The team has been working on the same lever for 12+ months without re-evaluating whether it is still the highest-impact opportunity.

### 7. Retention Is Not Neglected for Acquisition
- **Pass:** Even when acquisition is the focus lever, retention health is monitored. If retention is poor (leaky bucket), the team acknowledges that acquisition investment is partially wasted.
- **Fail:** The team pours resources into acquisition while retention degrades, filling and emptying the bucket simultaneously.

### 8. Referral/Virality Is Evaluated Honestly
- **Pass:** Referral is evaluated based on measured viral coefficient and cycle time, not aspirational hopes. If the product is not inherently viral, the team acknowledges this and does not force it.
- **Fail:** The team builds referral programs for products with no natural sharing behavior, wasting effort.

### 9. Monetization Lever Is Not Overlooked
- **Pass:** Pricing and packaging experiments are considered as growth levers. Revenue per user improvement is in the experiment backlog.
- **Fail:** Monetization is considered "not growth" and is never experimented on by the growth team.

### 10. Lever Focus Is Communicated to Stakeholders
- **Pass:** Leadership and adjacent teams understand which lever the growth team is focused on and why. Expectations are set about what will and will not be worked on.
- **Fail:** Stakeholders expect the growth team to work on all levers simultaneously, leading to context-switching and diluted impact.
