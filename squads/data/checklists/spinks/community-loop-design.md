# Community Loop Design

## Purpose

Audit whether the community has a functioning engagement loop: content attracts members, conversation deepens engagement, contribution creates value, and returning members sustain the cycle. Spinks emphasizes that communities without intentional loop design depend on heroic effort rather than systemic momentum.

---

## Checklist Items

### 1. The Engagement Loop Is Documented
- **Pass:** The community team has a written model of the engagement loop with each stage defined: discovery/attraction, first engagement, deepening participation, contribution, and return triggers.
- **Fail:** The team cannot articulate the loop. Community growth depends on "post things and hope people show up."

### 2. Content Seeds Conversation
- **Pass:** The community regularly produces seed content (discussion prompts, articles, challenges, AMAs) designed to start conversations, not just broadcast information. Seed content frequency is consistent.
- **Fail:** Content is one-directional (announcements, blog posts) with no conversation design. Members consume but do not interact.

### 3. Conversation Converts to Contribution
- **Pass:** There are designed pathways from conversation to contribution: a member who replies to a discussion is nudged to create their own post, share a resource, or answer a question.
- **Fail:** Members engage in conversation but are never invited or guided to contribute their own content or expertise.

### 4. Contribution Creates Value for Other Members
- **Pass:** Member contributions (answers, resources, tutorials, templates) are visible, searchable, and useful to other members. Contributed content becomes a community asset.
- **Fail:** Contributions disappear into a feed with no curation, discoverability, or persistence.

### 5. Return Triggers Are Designed
- **Pass:** Members receive relevant notifications, digests, or prompts that bring them back to the community based on their interests and past engagement (not mass emails to everyone).
- **Fail:** There are no re-engagement mechanisms, or members receive generic "you have notifications" emails that do not reference relevant content.

### 6. Each Loop Stage Has a Conversion Rate
- **Pass:** The team measures conversion between loop stages: visitor-to-member, member-to-first-engagement, first-engagement-to-repeat, repeat-to-contributor. Drop-off points are identified.
- **Fail:** The loop exists conceptually but no stage-to-stage conversion is measured.

### 7. New Member Onboarding Enters the Loop
- **Pass:** New member onboarding is explicitly designed to insert them into the engagement loop: welcome post, first interaction prompt, early contribution opportunity.
- **Fail:** New members join and receive no guidance on how to participate. They land on a feed and must figure it out.

### 8. The Loop Self-Reinforces
- **Pass:** The loop has built-in reinforcement: contributors receive recognition (likes, replies, badges), which motivates more contribution. The system generates its own energy.
- **Fail:** The loop requires constant manual effort from the community team to keep running. Without staff posting, the community goes silent.

### 9. Loop Health Is Monitored
- **Pass:** The community team has a dashboard or regular review showing the loop's health: are enough conversations starting, are they generating contributions, are members returning?
- **Fail:** Loop health is assessed anecdotally ("it feels active") rather than with data.

### 10. The Loop Has Been Iterated
- **Pass:** The community team has tested at least one change to the loop design in the last quarter (new content format, different onboarding sequence, new return trigger) and measured the impact.
- **Fail:** The loop design has not changed since launch. No experimentation has occurred.
