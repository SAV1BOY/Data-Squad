# Community Impact Attribution

## Purpose

Audit whether community impact on business outcomes (retention, expansion, support deflection) is honestly measured and attributed. Spinks acknowledges that community attribution is hard, but "hard" is not an excuse for "not attempted." This checklist ensures attribution is rigorous without overstating community's contribution.

---

## Checklist Items

### 1. Community Members Are Identifiable in Business Systems
- **Pass:** Community members can be matched to their records in CRM, product analytics, and support systems. A unique identifier links community activity to business outcomes.
- **Fail:** Community members exist in a separate system with no link to business data, making attribution impossible.

### 2. A Control Group Exists for Comparison
- **Pass:** Business outcomes (retention, NRR, support ticket volume, expansion rate) are compared between community members and non-members with similar characteristics. Selection bias is acknowledged.
- **Fail:** Community impact is claimed by comparing community members to all non-members without controlling for the fact that community joiners may be inherently more engaged.

### 3. Retention Impact Is Measured
- **Pass:** Retention rates for community members vs. matched non-members are calculated. The delta is reported with appropriate caveats about causation vs. correlation.
- **Fail:** Retention impact is assumed ("community members retain better, obviously") without measurement.

### 4. Support Deflection Is Quantified
- **Pass:** The number of support questions answered within the community (that would otherwise have been support tickets) is estimated. The cost savings are calculated.
- **Fail:** Support deflection is claimed but not measured. Nobody has estimated how many tickets the community prevents.

### 5. Expansion and Upsell Influence Is Tracked
- **Pass:** Expansion events (upsells, cross-sells) are analyzed for community touchpoints. Did the customer participate in the community before expanding? How does expansion rate differ for community members?
- **Fail:** Community's role in expansion is anecdotal ("a customer mentioned the community in their expansion call") rather than systematically tracked.

### 6. Attribution Is Honest About Limitations
- **Pass:** Attribution reports include caveats: self-selection bias (engaged customers join communities AND retain better), contribution vs. causation, and what the data cannot prove.
- **Fail:** Community is credited with causing outcomes that it may merely correlate with. The report is advocacy, not analysis.

### 7. Multiple Attribution Methods Are Considered
- **Pass:** The team uses at least two methods: (a) member vs. non-member comparison with matching, and (b) dose-response analysis (does more community engagement correlate with better outcomes?).
- **Fail:** Only one simplistic comparison is used, providing a single unreliable estimate.

### 8. Qualitative Attribution Supplements Quantitative
- **Pass:** Customer testimonials, exit survey mentions, NPS comments, and case studies provide qualitative evidence of community impact alongside the quantitative analysis.
- **Fail:** Only quantitative analysis is used, missing the rich context that qualitative data provides (or only qualitative is used, providing no rigor).

### 9. Attribution Findings Are Shared with Stakeholders
- **Pass:** Community impact analysis is regularly shared with leadership, marketing, CS, and product teams. The community team can articulate its value contribution in business terms.
- **Fail:** Attribution analysis is internal to the community team and never reaches decision-makers who control community investment.

### 10. Attribution Methodology Is Reviewed Annually
- **Pass:** The attribution approach is reviewed annually to incorporate better data sources, improved matching techniques, or new methods. The team is honest about what has improved and what is still uncertain.
- **Fail:** The same attribution methodology (or lack thereof) is used indefinitely without improvement.
