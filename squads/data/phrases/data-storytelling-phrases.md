# Data Storytelling Phrases

## Purpose

Ready-to-use phrases for building narratives around data findings. Organized by the stage of the story: setup, discovery, turning point, and resolution.

## Setting the Scene

- "To understand what happened, we need to start with where things stood in [time period]."
- "For the past [X] months, [metric] has been [stable/growing/declining] at approximately [value]."
- "The question we set out to answer was: [specific question]."
- "The context behind these numbers is important. At the time, [business context]."
- "On the surface, everything looked normal. But when we broke the data down by [segment], a different picture emerged."

## Introducing the Discovery

- "The data reveals something we did not expect."
- "What stands out in this analysis is [specific finding]."
- "When we segmented the data by [dimension], a clear pattern emerged."
- "The turning point came on [date], when [event or change] shifted the trajectory."
- "The numbers tell one story at the aggregate level and a completely different one at the segment level."
- "Hidden beneath the overall trend is a divergence between [group A] and [group B]."

## Building the Narrative

- "The trend accelerated after [event], growing from [value] to [value] in [time period]."
- "To put this in perspective, [comparison that makes the number meaningful]."
- "What changed was not [obvious factor] but [underlying factor], which shifted the dynamic."
- "This is not a one-time anomaly. The pattern has repeated across [X] consecutive [periods]."
- "The data tells us not just what happened, but when and where it started."
- "Each cohort shows the same pattern, which rules out one-off events and points to a structural change."

## Connecting to Impact

- "This matters because [business impact with quantified consequence]."
- "At the current trajectory, this will [projected outcome] by [date]."
- "The gap between where we are and where we planned to be represents [quantified impact]."
- "For every [unit of change in metric], we see [corresponding change in business outcome]."
- "The cost of not addressing this is approximately [value] per [time period]."

## Delivering the Resolution

- "Based on this analysis, the clearest path forward is [recommended action]."
- "The data points to one primary lever: [specific lever]."
- "We tested this hypothesis, and the results confirm [finding]. Here is what we recommend."
- "There are three options, and the data most strongly supports [option], because [reason]."
- "The good news is that this is addressable. [Specific action] is expected to [quantified improvement]."
- "This is a story of [concise theme: e.g., 'a product change that quietly shifted user behavior']."

## Transition Phrases

- "Now let us look at why this is happening."
- "The next question is naturally: what caused this?"
- "With that context, here is what the most recent data shows."
- "Before we jump to conclusions, there is an important nuance."
- "That brings us to the recommendation."
- "Zooming out from the detail, the broader takeaway is this."
