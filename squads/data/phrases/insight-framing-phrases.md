# Insight Framing Phrases

## Purpose

Standardized phrases for structuring insights using the pattern: observation, significance, and action. Every insight should answer three questions: What did we see? Why does it matter? What should we do?

## Stating the Observation

- "We observed that [metric] [changed/remained stable] by [amount] over [time period]."
- "The data shows a [clear/emerging/unexpected] pattern in [area]."
- "When we analyzed [data set], the most notable finding was [specific finding]."
- "A comparison of [segment A] and [segment B] reveals that [difference]."
- "[Metric] has [crossed a threshold / reached a new high / declined to a new low] as of [date]."
- "The analysis identified [X] distinct [patterns/segments/clusters] in the data."

## Explaining Why It Matters

- "This matters because [direct business consequence]."
- "The implication for our [goal/OKR/strategy] is [specific impact]."
- "If this trend continues, the expected impact is [quantified outcome] by [date]."
- "This is significant because it [contradicts our assumption / confirms our hypothesis / reveals a new opportunity]."
- "The reason this deserves attention is [specific reason tied to business priority]."
- "This represents a [risk/opportunity] of approximately [quantified value]."
- "Compared to our target of [value], this puts us [ahead/behind] by [amount]."

## Recommending Action

- "The recommended action is [specific action] by [owner] by [date]."
- "Based on this finding, we recommend [action] as the highest-impact next step."
- "There are two paths forward: [option A] or [option B]. We recommend [choice] because [reason]."
- "No immediate action is required, but we should monitor [metric] weekly and revisit if [condition]."
- "This insight supports [previously proposed initiative]. We recommend accelerating it."
- "The action is to [specific action]. Expected impact: [quantified estimate]."
- "We recommend investigating [specific area] further before deciding. Estimated time: [duration]."

## Connecting Insights to Each Other

- "This finding is consistent with [previous finding], reinforcing our confidence in [conclusion]."
- "In contrast to [previous finding], this suggests that [revised understanding]."
- "Combined with [other data point], this paints a picture of [broader narrative]."
- "This is the third consecutive [period] where we have seen this pattern, making it a trend rather than an anomaly."

## Framing Negative Findings

- "This is not the result we hoped for, but it is a clear signal that [interpretation]."
- "While the outcome was negative, it eliminates [hypothesis] and narrows our focus to [remaining options]."
- "The decline in [metric] is concentrated in [segment], which suggests a targeted intervention rather than a systemic issue."
- "This setback is recoverable. The data suggests [specific recovery path]."

## Framing Positive Findings

- "This is a strong result that validates [initiative/hypothesis]."
- "The improvement in [metric] directly contributes to [business goal] by [quantified amount]."
- "This success is attributable to [specific cause], which we can replicate in [other area]."
- "The positive trend has been sustained for [duration], making it reliable enough to factor into planning."
