# CS Playbook Effectiveness Checklist

## Purpose

Measure whether customer success playbooks (structured sequences of actions for specific scenarios) actually improve outcomes. Playbooks are only useful if they produce better results than unstructured CSM judgment. This gate separates effective playbooks from bureaucratic theater.

---

## Checklist Items

### 1. Each Playbook Has a Measurable Outcome Metric

- **Pass:** Every playbook specifies the outcome it aims to achieve and the metric that measures it. Examples: onboarding playbook measures time-to-value and Day-30 activation rate; escalation playbook measures time-to-resolution and save rate.
- **Fail:** The playbook exists as a process document with steps to follow but no defined success metric.

### 2. Playbook Adherence Is Tracked

- **Pass:** The CS platform (or equivalent) logs which playbook steps were completed, skipped, or modified for each account engagement. Adherence rate is computed per CSM and per playbook.
- **Fail:** Playbooks exist in a wiki but there is no way to know if CSMs follow them.

### 3. Outcomes Are Compared Between Adhered and Non-Adhered Engagements

- **Pass:** Accounts where the playbook was followed are compared to accounts where it was not (or where it was partially followed). The comparison controls for account characteristics (size, segment, risk level) to isolate the playbook's contribution.
- **Fail:** No comparison exists; the team assumes playbooks work because they seem logical.

### 4. Playbook Variants Are Tested

- **Pass:** When the team hypothesizes a playbook improvement (different cadence, different content, different channel), an A/B test or sequential test is run to measure whether the variant outperforms the current version.
- **Fail:** Playbooks are updated based on anecdote ("I tried this and it seemed to work") without controlled measurement.

### 5. CSM Feedback Is Systematically Collected

- **Pass:** CSMs provide structured feedback on each playbook quarterly: which steps are valuable, which are friction, which are irrelevant for certain segments. Feedback is reviewed and acted upon.
- **Fail:** CSMs silently skip steps they find unhelpful, or feedback is collected but never acted on.

### 6. Playbooks Are Segmented Appropriately

- **Pass:** Different customer segments (enterprise vs. SMB, technical vs. non-technical, high-touch vs. low-touch) have distinct playbooks or playbook branches. One-size-fits-all playbooks are avoided.
- **Fail:** A single onboarding playbook is used for a 5-person startup and a 10,000-person enterprise.

### 7. Underperforming Playbooks Are Retired or Rebuilt

- **Pass:** Playbooks that show no measurable improvement over unstructured engagement (after controlling for confounders) are either redesigned with new hypotheses or retired. A quarterly review cadence ensures stale playbooks do not persist.
- **Fail:** Playbooks accumulate indefinitely; no playbook has ever been retired.

### 8. Time Investment vs. Outcome Is Balanced

- **Pass:** The total CSM time required per playbook execution is tracked. Playbooks that consume excessive time relative to their outcome impact are simplified or automated where possible.
- **Fail:** A playbook requires 8 hours of CSM time per account but produces marginal improvement over a 2-hour unstructured engagement.
