# Adoption Milestones Checklist

## Purpose

Define and track the key product usage milestones that indicate a customer is extracting increasing value. These milestones go beyond onboarding and map the journey from initial activation to deep, sticky adoption that predicts expansion and renewal.

---

## Checklist Items

### 1. Milestones Are Mapped to the Value Journey

- **Pass:** The team has defined 3-5 sequential adoption milestones that represent increasing depth of product usage (e.g., Milestone 1: first integration live; Milestone 2: 5 active users; Milestone 3: first automated workflow; Milestone 4: cross-department expansion). Each milestone has a clear behavioral definition.
- **Fail:** Milestones are vague ("customer is engaged") or only one milestone (onboarding) exists.

### 2. Each Milestone Is Validated Against Retention Data

- **Pass:** Retrospective analysis shows that customers who reach each successive milestone have statistically higher renewal rates and lower churn probability. The lift between milestones is quantified.
- **Fail:** Milestones were chosen intuitively ("seems important") without validating their predictive power.

### 3. Milestone Achievement Is Tracked Automatically

- **Pass:** Product usage data flows into the CS platform or data warehouse, and milestone status is computed automatically based on behavioral triggers. CSMs can see each account's current milestone in their dashboard.
- **Fail:** Milestone tracking depends on the CSM manually logging it based on their perception of the account.

### 4. Time Between Milestones Is Benchmarked

- **Pass:** The median and P75 time between consecutive milestones is established per segment. Accounts that exceed the P75 benchmark are flagged as stalled and receive proactive intervention.
- **Fail:** No time benchmarks exist; an account that has been stuck at Milestone 1 for 6 months receives no escalation.

### 5. Blockers at Each Milestone Are Cataloged

- **Pass:** The most common reasons customers stall at each milestone are documented (e.g., "stalls at Milestone 2 because SSO integration is required for bulk user onboarding"). Blockers are prioritized with product and engineering.
- **Fail:** The team knows some accounts are stuck but has not systematically identified the recurring blockers.

### 6. CSM Playbooks Align to Milestones

- **Pass:** Each milestone transition has a corresponding CSM playbook: specific actions, talk tracks, resources, and success criteria. The playbook is triggered when an account reaches the appropriate stage.
- **Fail:** CSMs follow a one-size-fits-all engagement cadence regardless of where the account is in its adoption journey.

### 7. Milestone Data Informs Expansion Opportunities

- **Pass:** Accounts that reach advanced milestones (deep adoption, cross-department usage) are flagged as expansion candidates. The CS and sales teams have a shared view of milestone-based expansion signals.
- **Fail:** Expansion targets are selected based on contract size or renewal date alone, ignoring usage-based signals.

### 8. Milestone Definitions Are Reviewed Annually

- **Pass:** As the product evolves, milestones are reassessed. New features may create new value paths; deprecated features may invalidate old milestones. The review includes fresh correlation analysis against retention.
- **Fail:** Milestones were defined two years ago and still reference features that have been redesigned or removed.
