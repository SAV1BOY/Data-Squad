# Community Data Tone Language Guide

## Purpose

This guide defines how the data team communicates community metrics, engagement data, and behavioral insights about user communities. The audience includes community managers, marketing, product teams, and occasionally the community members themselves. Community data is inherently human and must be treated with care.

## Core Principles

1. **People, not data points**: Community members are people; language should reflect that
2. **Engagement over vanity**: Focus on meaningful participation, not raw counts
3. **Context-rich**: Community metrics need qualitative context more than most data
4. **Trend-oriented**: Point-in-time community numbers are less useful than trajectories
5. **Respectful of privacy**: Aggregate always; never surface individual behavior without clear consent and purpose

## Communication Contexts

### Internal Reporting
- Monthly community health reviews
- Engagement trend analysis
- Community-driven feature adoption signals
- Advocate and champion identification (aggregated patterns)
- Content performance and topic analysis

### Community-Facing
- Transparency reports and community updates
- Annual community surveys and results
- Milestone celebrations with data

## Language Patterns

| Instead of | Use |
|---|---|
| "We tracked 5,000 community users" | "5,000 community members participated in discussions this month" |
| "Power users generate 80% of content" | "A dedicated group of active contributors drives the majority of community discussions" |
| "Engagement is down" | "Participation in community discussions decreased 12% MoM, concentrated in the help-and-support category" |
| "We are monitoring user behavior" | "We review aggregated community activity patterns to improve the experience" |
| "Low-value members" | "Members who have not yet found their community niche" |
| "Lurkers" | "Community readers" or "Observing members" |

## Metrics That Matter for Community

- **Active contributors**: Members who posted, commented, or reacted in the period
- **Discussion depth**: Average replies per thread (quality signal)
- **Time to first response**: How quickly new members get help
- **Return participation rate**: Members who contribute more than once
- **Topic diversity**: Breadth of subjects being discussed
- **New member activation**: Percentage of new joiners who participate within 30 days

## Privacy and Ethics Guidelines

- Never report on identifiable individuals without explicit purpose and approval
- Aggregate to groups of at least 25 before reporting behavioral patterns
- Do not use community data to build individual risk or churn profiles without disclosure
- Community sentiment data should be handled with the same care as NPS or CSAT
- When sharing community data externally, obtain appropriate review

## Formatting for Community Reports

- Lead with the community health headline: thriving, stable, needs attention
- Include one human story or example alongside the numbers (anonymized)
- Use charts that show trends, not just snapshots
- Celebrate positive milestones with specific attribution (with consent)
- Keep internal jargon out of community-facing reports

## Common Mistakes

- Treating community metrics with the same detachment as product metrics
- Reporting member counts as a success metric without engagement context
- Using surveillance-adjacent language when describing community analytics
- Ignoring qualitative signals that the quantitative data cannot capture
- Comparing communities to competitors without accounting for maturity stage
- Presenting declining engagement without exploring the reasons why
