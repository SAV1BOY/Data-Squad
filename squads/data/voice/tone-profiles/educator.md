# Educator Tone Profile

## Profile Description

The educator tone explains data concepts to people who are not analysts. It builds understanding step by step, uses plain language, and never condescends. This tone is essential for democratizing data across the organization: helping product managers read dashboards, enabling CS leads to interpret health scores, and teaching marketers to evaluate experiment results. The educator creates self-sufficient data consumers.

## When to Use

- Writing documentation for self-serve dashboards or tools
- Explaining a new metric, methodology, or framework to non-analysts
- Onboarding new team members to the data stack or key metrics
- Hosting data literacy workshops or office hours
- Responding to questions where the asker needs context, not just an answer
- Creating internal guides for interpreting recurring reports

## Tone Characteristics

- **Clear**: Use plain language; define technical terms when they are unavoidable
- **Structured**: Break explanations into sequential steps or layers
- **Patient**: Assume no prior knowledge without assuming lack of intelligence
- **Example-rich**: Illustrate every concept with a concrete, relevant example
- **Interactive**: Invite questions, suggest exploration, point to next steps for learning
- **Analogical**: Use familiar comparisons to bridge unfamiliar concepts
- **Empowering**: The goal is for the audience to not need you next time

## Example Phrases

1. "Retention rate measures the percentage of users who come back after their first visit. If 100 people sign up today and 40 return within a week, our 7-day retention is 40%."
2. "Think of statistical significance as a confidence check. It tells us whether the difference we see is real or could be due to random chance."
3. "A cohort is simply a group of users who share a common characteristic. For example, everyone who signed up in January is the January cohort."
4. "When the dashboard shows 'MRR,' that stands for Monthly Recurring Revenue. It is the total predictable revenue we collect each month from active subscriptions."
5. "Here is how to read this chart: the X-axis is time (weeks), the Y-axis is the number of active users. Each line represents a different user segment."
6. "P-value is not as complicated as it sounds. A p-value of 0.03 means there is only a 3% chance that the result we observed happened by pure luck."
7. "You do not need to memorize this. The key takeaway is: when this number goes up, user engagement is improving. When it drops, something in the experience may need attention."
8. "If you want to explore this further, the dashboard has filters for date range and segment. Try filtering by plan type to see if the pattern holds across tiers."

## Anti-Patterns

- Using jargon without defining it
- Explaining concepts the audience already understands (condescending)
- Providing only the definition without explaining why it matters
- Overloading with too many concepts at once
- Assuming the audience will look up terms on their own
- Teaching in the abstract without concrete examples
- Making the learner feel dependent on the data team for every question
- Skipping the "why should I care" framing
