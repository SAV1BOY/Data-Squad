# Moderation Runbook Checklist

## Purpose

Ensure the community has clear, consistent moderation practices that protect members, maintain constructive discourse, and handle incidents without arbitrary or biased enforcement. This gate prevents both under-moderation (toxicity drives members away) and over-moderation (censorship chills participation).

---

## Checklist Items

### 1. Community Guidelines Are Published and Accessible

- **Pass:** Clear, specific community guidelines are posted prominently (pinned post, sidebar, onboarding flow). Guidelines cover: acceptable behavior, prohibited behavior (with examples), consequences, and the appeals process.
- **Fail:** Guidelines are vague ("be nice"), buried in a terms-of-service document, or do not exist.

### 2. Moderation Actions Are Tiered and Proportional

- **Pass:** A defined escalation ladder exists: warning, content removal, temporary mute, temporary ban, permanent ban. Each tier maps to specific violation types. First-time minor offenses do not receive the same treatment as repeated or severe violations.
- **Fail:** Moderators jump straight to bans, or enforcement is inconsistent (same offense gets a warning for one person and a ban for another).

### 3. Moderators Are Trained and Calibrated

- **Pass:** All moderators (staff and volunteer) complete a training that includes: guideline interpretation, bias awareness, de-escalation techniques, and practice scenarios. Calibration sessions (reviewing the same case) happen quarterly.
- **Fail:** Moderation is assigned to whoever is available, with no training or calibration.

### 4. Incident Response Has a Defined Protocol

- **Pass:** For serious incidents (harassment campaigns, doxxing, legal threats, coordinated raids), a documented protocol specifies: who is notified, within what timeframe, what immediate actions are taken (lock thread, ban accounts), and how affected members are supported.
- **Fail:** Serious incidents are handled ad-hoc by whoever sees them first.

### 5. Appeals Process Is Functional

- **Pass:** Moderated members can appeal through a defined channel (form, email, specific thread). Appeals are reviewed by a different moderator than the one who took the original action. Response time target is 72 hours.
- **Fail:** No appeals process exists, or appeals go to the same moderator who made the initial decision.

### 6. Moderation Log Is Maintained

- **Pass:** Every moderation action (warning, removal, mute, ban) is logged with: the content/behavior that triggered it, the guideline violated, the action taken, and the moderator responsible. The log is auditable by community leadership.
- **Fail:** Moderation actions are not recorded; there is no way to review decisions or detect patterns.

### 7. Moderator Well-Being Is Considered

- **Pass:** Moderators (especially those handling toxic or disturbing content) have access to rotation schedules to prevent burnout, clear guidance on when to escalate rather than handle alone, and periodic check-ins with management.
- **Fail:** The same person handles all toxic content indefinitely with no support structure.

### 8. Moderation Metrics Are Reviewed Monthly

- **Pass:** Monthly review covers: number of moderation actions by type, response time to reports, appeal rate, appeal overturn rate, and moderator workload distribution. Trends inform staffing and guideline updates.
- **Fail:** No one tracks whether moderation is working; problems are only visible when a public blowup occurs.
