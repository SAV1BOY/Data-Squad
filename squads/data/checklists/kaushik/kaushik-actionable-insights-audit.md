# Kaushik Actionable Insights Audit

## Purpose

Distinguish between "interesting data" and genuinely actionable insights. An actionable insight identifies what happened, explains why it matters, and recommends a specific action. If analysis stops at "here's what happened," it has not yet become an insight. This checklist enforces Kaushik's standard for analysis quality.

---

## Checklist Items

### 1. Every Analysis Ends with a Recommendation
- **Pass:** Each analysis deliverable (report, deck, email) concludes with at least one specific, implementable recommendation tied to the findings.
- **Fail:** Analysis ends with "here are the numbers" and leaves interpretation and action entirely to the reader.

### 2. Insights Follow the What-Why-Action Structure
- **Pass:** Each insight is structured as: (1) What happened (the observation), (2) Why it matters (the business impact), (3) What to do about it (the recommended action).
- **Fail:** Insights are observations only ("bounce rate increased 10%") with no explanation or recommendation.

### 3. The "Interesting" vs. "Actionable" Distinction Is Explicit
- **Pass:** The team has a shared definition: "interesting" means notable but no action follows; "actionable" means a specific person can do a specific thing. Only actionable findings are labeled as insights.
- **Fail:** Everything in the data is called an "insight," diluting the term to meaninglessness.

### 4. Insights Are Prioritized by Business Impact
- **Pass:** When multiple insights exist, they are ranked by estimated revenue impact, cost savings, or strategic value. The highest-impact insight leads.
- **Fail:** Insights are presented in random order or in the order they were discovered.

### 5. Insights Specify the Owner of the Recommended Action
- **Pass:** Each recommendation names the team or individual responsible for acting on it (e.g., "The landing page team should test a shorter form").
- **Fail:** Recommendations are addressed to nobody, making follow-through unlikely.

### 6. Insights Are Tested, Not Just Asserted
- **Pass:** Where possible, insights include supporting evidence from multiple data sources, statistical significance, or A/B test results.
- **Fail:** Insights are based on a single metric movement with no corroborating evidence or significance testing.

### 7. Follow-Up on Previous Insights Is Documented
- **Pass:** There is a log tracking past insights, whether they were acted upon, and what the outcome was. This log is reviewed monthly.
- **Fail:** Insights are delivered and forgotten. Nobody tracks whether they were implemented or what happened.

### 8. Analysis Cadence Includes Proactive Exploration
- **Pass:** At least 30% of analysis time is spent on proactive exploration (looking for patterns, anomalies, opportunities) rather than answering ad hoc requests.
- **Fail:** 100% of analysis is reactive -- analysts only investigate when asked a specific question.

### 9. Insights Are Communicated in Business Language
- **Pass:** Insights are written in terms the audience understands, using business metrics and plain language. Technical jargon is translated.
- **Fail:** Insights are full of statistical terminology, tool-specific language, or data jargon that the audience cannot parse.

### 10. The Insight-to-Action Conversion Rate Is Tracked
- **Pass:** The team measures what percentage of delivered insights result in implemented actions within 30 days. The target is above 50%.
- **Fail:** Nobody measures whether insights drive action, making it impossible to know if the analysis function is delivering value.
