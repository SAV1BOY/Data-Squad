# Mehta Digital CS Audit

## Purpose

Audit the digital customer success program: how CS scales through data-driven automation, self-service resources, and tech-touch engagement. Mehta argues that digital CS is not a cost-cutting measure but a way to deliver consistent, proactive value to customers who cannot (or do not need to) have a dedicated CSM.

---

## Checklist Items

### 1. Digital CS Has a Defined Scope and Audience
- **Pass:** There is a clear definition of which customer segments receive digital-led CS (typically SMB, self-serve, or lower-tier accounts) and what the digital CS program covers.
- **Fail:** Digital CS is undefined. Lower-tier customers receive no CS at all, or all customers receive the same high-touch model regardless of value.

### 2. Automated Onboarding Sequences Exist
- **Pass:** New customers in the digital segment receive automated onboarding sequences (email, in-app guides, video tutorials) that guide them toward activation. Sequences are triggered by signup and progress events.
- **Fail:** Digital-segment customers get a welcome email and nothing else, leaving activation to chance.

### 3. In-App Guidance Is Data-Driven
- **Pass:** In-app messages, tooltips, and guides are triggered by user behavior (e.g., "User has not completed setup step 3 after 48 hours") rather than shown to all users regardless of context.
- **Fail:** In-app guidance is a static tour shown once at first login with no behavioral triggers.

### 4. Self-Service Resources Are Comprehensive and Maintained
- **Pass:** A knowledge base, help center, or documentation site covers the top 20 support topics. Content is reviewed and updated at least quarterly. Search functionality is effective.
- **Fail:** Help content is sparse, outdated, or hard to find. Customers cannot self-serve basic questions.

### 5. Health Monitoring Extends to Digital Accounts
- **Pass:** Health scores or risk signals are computed for digital-segment accounts, even though they do not have a dedicated CSM. Automated alerts trigger interventions for at-risk accounts.
- **Fail:** Only accounts with dedicated CSMs have health scores. Digital-segment accounts churn silently.

### 6. Lifecycle Campaigns Are Mapped and Active
- **Pass:** Automated campaigns are mapped to the customer lifecycle: onboarding, adoption deepening, value realization, renewal preparation, and win-back. Each stage has active, tested campaigns.
- **Fail:** The only automated touchpoints are the welcome email and the renewal notice.

### 7. Digital CS Metrics Are Tracked
- **Pass:** The digital CS program has its own metrics: email engagement rates, in-app guide completion, self-service deflection rate, digital-segment NRR, and activation rate.
- **Fail:** The digital CS program runs but nobody measures whether it is working.

### 8. Escalation Paths to Human CS Exist
- **Pass:** When automated signals detect that a digital-segment customer needs human attention (high-value expansion opportunity, critical issue, champion engagement), there is a defined escalation path to a CSM or pooled CS team.
- **Fail:** Digital-segment customers have no way to reach a human, even when they have a problem automation cannot solve.

### 9. Content Effectiveness Is Measured
- **Pass:** Automated emails, guides, and resources are A/B tested or at least measured for engagement and impact on desired behavior. Low-performing content is improved or retired.
- **Fail:** Content is created and deployed without measuring whether it influences customer behavior.

### 10. Digital and Human CS Are Coordinated
- **Pass:** When a digital-segment customer upgrades to a higher tier (or vice versa), the transition between digital and human CS is smooth. Context from digital interactions is available to the CSM.
- **Fail:** The transition from digital to human CS loses all context. The CSM starts from scratch with no history.
