# DMMM Measurement Model Audit

## Purpose

Audit the Digital Marketing and Measurement Model (DMMM) as defined by Avinash Kaushik. Every digital initiative must have a clear chain: Business Objective -> Goal -> KPI -> Target. This checklist validates that the chain is complete, internally consistent, and free of vanity metrics masquerading as KPIs.

---

## Checklist Items

### 1. Business Objective Exists and Is Clearly Stated
- **Pass:** A plain-language business objective is documented (e.g., "Increase online revenue by expanding customer base").
- **Fail:** Objective is missing, vague ("do better online"), or a restatement of a tactic.

### 2. Goals Are Specific and Tied to Objective
- **Pass:** Each objective has 2-5 concrete goals that, if achieved, would demonstrably move the objective (e.g., "Increase conversion rate for new visitors").
- **Fail:** Goals are generic ("improve engagement"), overlap significantly, or have no logical link to the objective.

### 3. KPIs Are Quantifiable and Owned
- **Pass:** Every goal has 1-3 KPIs that are numeric, measurable with current instrumentation, and have a named owner.
- **Fail:** KPIs are unmeasurable, unowned, or more than three per goal (sign of unfocused thinking).

### 4. Targets Are Set with Rationale
- **Pass:** Each KPI has a numeric target with a documented basis (historical trend, benchmark, or business requirement) and a time horizon.
- **Fail:** Targets are missing, arbitrary round numbers with no justification, or have no deadline.

### 5. No Vanity Metrics in the KPI Layer
- **Pass:** Every KPI passes Kaushik's "so what?" test -- if the KPI moves, a clear business action follows.
- **Fail:** KPIs include raw page views, total visits, or social followers without context or actionability.

### 6. Segments Are Identified
- **Pass:** The model identifies at least two meaningful audience segments (e.g., new vs. returning, high-value vs. low-value) and maps KPIs per segment.
- **Fail:** All users are treated as a monolith with no segmentation strategy.

### 7. Micro and Macro Conversions Are Distinct
- **Pass:** Macro conversions (revenue events) and micro conversions (engagement signals like newsletter sign-ups) are separately tracked and valued.
- **Fail:** Only macro conversions are measured, or micro and macro are conflated.

### 8. Economic Value Is Assigned to Micro Conversions
- **Pass:** Each micro conversion has a documented economic value based on downstream conversion probability or business estimate.
- **Fail:** Micro conversions have no value assignment, making ROI analysis impossible.

### 9. Model Is Reviewed on a Defined Cadence
- **Pass:** There is a quarterly (or more frequent) review of the DMMM to retire stale KPIs and add new ones as strategy evolves.
- **Fail:** The model was created once and never revisited, or there is no review schedule.

### 10. Stakeholder Alignment Is Documented
- **Pass:** Key stakeholders (marketing lead, analytics lead, executive sponsor) have signed off on the model, and sign-off is recorded.
- **Fail:** The model exists only in the analyst's notebook with no stakeholder buy-in.
