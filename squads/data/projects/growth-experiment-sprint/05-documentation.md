# Growth Experiment Sprint - Documentation

## Phase Objective

Capture all learnings from the experiment sprint, update the organizational knowledge base, refine the experimentation process, and seed the hypothesis backlog for the next sprint. This phase ensures that the value of each experiment extends beyond its immediate result through accumulated organizational knowledge.

## Prerequisites

- Decisions finalized for all experiments from Phase 04
- Experiment registry updated with outcomes
- Rollout or rollback actions completed or scheduled
- Access to the learnings repository and hypothesis backlog
- Sprint retrospective scheduled with the growth team

## Steps

1. **Write Detailed Experiment Case Studies**: For each experiment, write a narrative case study that goes beyond the registry entry. Describe the full story: what prompted the hypothesis, what the data showed during analysis, what surprised the team, and what the result implies about user behavior. Case studies are more memorable and transferable than spreadsheet entries and help new team members absorb institutional knowledge quickly.

2. **Extract Generalizable Insights**: Review all experiment results from this sprint together. Identify patterns that transcend individual experiments. For example, if multiple experiments targeting the onboarding flow showed that reducing steps improved activation but hurt retention, document this tension as a strategic insight. These meta-learnings are often more valuable than individual experiment results.

3. **Update the Learnings Registry**: Add structured entries for each experiment to the central learnings registry. Each entry should include the hypothesis, treatment summary, primary metric result with confidence interval, guardrail outcomes, decision made, key insight, and tags for categorization. Ensure the registry is searchable so future teams can find relevant precedents before designing new experiments.

4. **Update User Behavior Mental Models**: Revise the team's understanding of how users behave based on experiment results. If an experiment revealed that users value simplicity over feature richness, or that price sensitivity varies dramatically by segment, update the documented assumptions that guide future hypothesis generation. These mental model updates compound over time and improve hypothesis quality.

5. **Refine the Growth Model**: Based on sprint results, update the growth model with any new conversion rates, segment behaviors, or funnel dynamics discovered. If a shipped experiment improved activation rate by 5%, update the model to reflect the new baseline. If an experiment revealed a previously unknown drop-off point, add it to the funnel map.

6. **Seed the Next Sprint Backlog**: Using insights from this sprint, generate new hypotheses for the next sprint. Shipped experiments often suggest follow-up optimizations. Killed experiments may point to alternative approaches. Segment analyses may reveal opportunities for targeted interventions. Add these hypotheses to the backlog with the evidence that supports them.

7. **Audit the Experimentation Process**: Review the sprint process itself for improvements. Evaluate whether the timeline was realistic, whether the statistical methodology was appropriate, whether the decision framework was applied consistently, and whether communication was effective. Identify specific process changes that would make the next sprint more efficient or rigorous.

8. **Conduct the Sprint Retrospective**: Gather the growth team for a structured retrospective. Discuss what went well (experiments that produced clear, actionable results), what went poorly (experiments that had to be restarted due to implementation bugs, or decisions that were overridden by opinion rather than data), and what to change for the next sprint. Document action items with owners and deadlines.

9. **Archive Sprint Materials**: Organize and archive all sprint materials in a structured location. This includes experiment briefs, design documents, analysis reports, decision documents, case studies, and the retrospective. Use a consistent folder structure and naming convention so materials can be found months or years later when someone encounters a similar problem.

## Deliverables

- Detailed case studies for each experiment in the sprint
- Generalizable insights document with cross-experiment patterns
- Updated learnings registry with complete entries for all experiments
- Revised growth model reflecting new baselines and discoveries
- Seeded hypothesis backlog for the next sprint with supporting evidence
- Process improvement recommendations with specific action items
- Sprint retrospective document with action items, owners, and deadlines
- Archived sprint materials in the standard folder structure

## Quality Gate

Documentation is complete when all of the following conditions are met:

- Every experiment has a case study that tells the full narrative beyond raw numbers
- The learnings registry is updated and searchable with complete entries
- The growth model reflects new baselines from shipped experiments
- At least five new hypotheses have been added to the backlog for the next sprint
- Process improvement recommendations have been reviewed and accepted by the team lead
- The sprint retrospective has been conducted and action items assigned
- All sprint materials are archived in the standard location and accessible to the team
