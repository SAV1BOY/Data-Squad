# Growth Experiment Velocity

## Purpose

Audit the pace, rigor, and learning output of the growth experimentation program. Ellis emphasizes that growth is a process of rapid experimentation -- the team that runs more quality experiments per unit of time learns faster and wins. This checklist measures whether the experimentation engine is healthy.

---

## Checklist Items

### 1. Experiment Velocity Is Tracked
- **Pass:** The team measures experiments launched per week (or sprint). The number is documented and trended. A target velocity exists (e.g., 2-3 experiments per week).
- **Fail:** Nobody counts experiments, or they are run sporadically with no cadence.

### 2. An Experiment Backlog Exists and Is Prioritized
- **Pass:** There is a maintained, prioritized backlog of experiment ideas with at least 3x the number that can be run in the next sprint. Ideas are scored (e.g., ICE or similar).
- **Fail:** Experiments are improvised week by week with no backlog, or the backlog exists but is not prioritized.

### 3. Each Experiment Has a Written Hypothesis
- **Pass:** Every experiment has a hypothesis in the format: "If we [change X], then [metric Y] will [move Z], because [rationale]." The hypothesis is written before the experiment launches.
- **Fail:** Experiments are launched without a stated hypothesis, making it impossible to determine what was learned.

### 4. Success Criteria Are Pre-Defined
- **Pass:** Before launch, each experiment defines the metric to measure, the minimum detectable effect, the sample size needed, and the runtime required.
- **Fail:** Success is judged after the fact by looking at whichever metric moved, introducing post-hoc rationalization.

### 5. Experiment Results Are Documented
- **Pass:** Every experiment (win, loss, or inconclusive) is logged in a shared repository with hypothesis, setup, results, and learnings. The repository is searchable.
- **Fail:** Results are shared in Slack and lost, or only wins are documented while losses disappear.

### 6. Learning Velocity Is Measured, Not Just Win Rate
- **Pass:** The team tracks learnings per week, not just win rate. A failed experiment that teaches something is valued. Win rate alone is not the optimization target.
- **Fail:** The team optimizes for win rate, leading to only safe, small experiments and no bold bets.

### 7. Experiments Cover Multiple Growth Levers
- **Pass:** The experiment portfolio spans acquisition, activation, retention, referral, and monetization. No single lever dominates the backlog without strategic justification.
- **Fail:** All experiments focus on one lever (usually acquisition) while other levers are neglected.

### 8. Experiment Turnaround Time Is Monitored
- **Pass:** The average time from idea to launched experiment is tracked and actively minimized. Bottlenecks (design, engineering, data) are identified and addressed.
- **Fail:** Good ideas sit in the backlog for months because of resource bottlenecks or process overhead.

### 9. Statistical Rigor Is Maintained
- **Pass:** Experiments use proper statistical methodology: appropriate sample sizes, significance thresholds (p < 0.05 or equivalent Bayesian criteria), and correction for multiple comparisons when needed.
- **Fail:** Experiments are called early, sample sizes are insufficient, or "directional" results are treated as conclusive.

### 10. Growth Meeting Reviews Experiments Weekly
- **Pass:** A weekly growth meeting reviews launched experiments, concluded experiments, and upcoming launches. The meeting has a consistent format and consistent attendance.
- **Fail:** There is no regular forum for experiment review, or it happens inconsistently.
