# Mehta Customer Outcome Audit

## Purpose

Audit whether CS is measuring customer results (outcomes) rather than CS activities (touchpoints). Mehta argues that the shift from activity-based CS ("we made 50 calls this week") to outcome-based CS ("15 customers hit their KPI target this quarter") is the defining evolution of modern customer success.

---

## Checklist Items

### 1. Customer Outcomes Are Defined per Segment or Use Case
- **Pass:** For each customer segment or primary use case, there is a documented definition of what "success" looks like in the customer's terms (e.g., "reduce time-to-close by 20%" or "achieve 95% uptime").
- **Fail:** Success is defined only in vendor terms (adoption, usage) with no connection to the customer's actual business goals.

### 2. Outcomes Are Captured at Onboarding
- **Pass:** During onboarding, the CSM captures the customer's specific success criteria: what they want to achieve, by when, and how they will measure it. This is documented in the CRM.
- **Fail:** Onboarding focuses on product setup without discussing what success means for this customer.

### 3. Outcome Metrics Are Measurable
- **Pass:** Customer outcomes are defined in measurable terms. If the outcome is "improve team productivity," there is a proxy metric (time per task, throughput, output per person) that can be tracked.
- **Fail:** Outcomes are aspirational statements ("be more efficient") that cannot be measured or validated.

### 4. Progress Toward Outcomes Is Tracked Over Time
- **Pass:** There is a system for tracking progress toward each customer's defined outcomes at regular intervals (monthly or quarterly). Progress is visible to both the CSM and the customer.
- **Fail:** Outcomes are captured at onboarding and then forgotten until the renewal conversation.

### 5. CS Activities Are Linked to Outcomes
- **Pass:** Each CS activity (onboarding call, training session, health check, QBR) has a documented purpose tied to driving a specific customer outcome.
- **Fail:** CS activities are performed on a schedule without connection to what they are meant to achieve for the customer.

### 6. Outcome Achievement Rate Is a CS KPI
- **Pass:** The CS team tracks what percentage of customers achieve their defined outcomes. This metric is reviewed regularly and informs CS strategy.
- **Fail:** CS KPIs are activity-based (calls made, QBRs completed, health checks performed) with no outcome measurement.

### 7. Unachieved Outcomes Are Investigated
- **Pass:** When a customer does not achieve their stated outcome, the CS team conducts a post-mortem to determine whether the gap was product capability, adoption failure, unrealistic goal, or something else.
- **Fail:** Outcome failure is ignored or blamed on the customer without investigation.

### 8. Outcome Data Feeds Product Development
- **Pass:** Patterns in unachieved outcomes (e.g., "customers wanting X outcome consistently fail because the product lacks Y capability") are communicated to the product team.
- **Fail:** CS and product do not share outcome data. Product builds features without knowing which customer outcomes are underserved.

### 9. Outcome Stories Are Used in Marketing and Sales
- **Pass:** Verified customer outcomes are packaged as case studies and reference stories, with customer permission. These stories are specific and quantified.
- **Fail:** Marketing uses generic testimonials rather than specific, measured outcome stories.

### 10. The Outcome Framework Evolves
- **Pass:** The set of tracked outcomes is reviewed annually to ensure it reflects current customer needs, product capabilities, and market conditions.
- **Fail:** The outcome framework was set once and does not reflect how the product and customer base have evolved.
