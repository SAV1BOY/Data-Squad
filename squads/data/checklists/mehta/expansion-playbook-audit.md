# Expansion Playbook Audit

## Purpose

Audit the upsell and cross-sell playbook for triggers, timing, ethical grounding, and ROI measurement. Mehta emphasizes that expansion should be a natural outcome of customer success -- not a pressure tactic. When customers achieve outcomes, expansion is a service; when they do not, it is a betrayal of trust.

---

## Checklist Items

### 1. Expansion Triggers Are Defined
- **Pass:** Specific, data-driven triggers are documented for expansion conversations (e.g., usage hitting 80% of plan limits, adoption of a feature that precedes upsell, positive health score trend, successful QBR outcome).
- **Fail:** Expansion attempts are calendar-driven ("it's renewal time, let's try to upsell") or random rather than signal-driven.

### 2. Timing Respects the Customer Journey
- **Pass:** Expansion is proposed only after the customer has achieved demonstrated value from their current product. There is a minimum value-realization threshold before expansion conversations begin.
- **Fail:** Expansion is pitched during onboarding or to customers who have not yet activated, damaging trust before it is built.

### 3. Customer Value Is the Lead, Not Revenue Targets
- **Pass:** Expansion conversations lead with additional value the customer will receive, not with the vendor's revenue need. The pitch answers "why this helps you" before "what this costs."
- **Fail:** Expansion feels like a sales push. CSMs are pressured to upsell regardless of whether it serves the customer.

### 4. CSM and Sales Roles Are Clear
- **Pass:** It is documented who identifies expansion opportunities (usually CSM), who leads the commercial conversation (CSM or account exec), and how handoffs work. Compensation incentives are aligned.
- **Fail:** CSMs and sales compete over expansion revenue, creating customer confusion and internal conflict.

### 5. Expansion Revenue Is Tracked Separately
- **Pass:** Expansion revenue (upsell, cross-sell, seat additions) is tracked as a distinct metric from new business revenue. NRR impact from expansion is isolated.
- **Fail:** Expansion revenue is lumped with new sales, making it impossible to measure the CS team's contribution.

### 6. Win Rate on Expansion Opportunities Is Measured
- **Pass:** The team tracks how many expansion opportunities are identified, how many are proposed, and how many close. Win rate and average deal size are monitored.
- **Fail:** There is no pipeline tracking for expansion. Opportunities are pursued ad hoc without measurement.

### 7. Customers Who Are Not Ready Are Protected
- **Pass:** There is an explicit rule: do not propose expansion to at-risk or unhappy customers. The health score or CSM judgment gates expansion eligibility.
- **Fail:** At-risk customers are targeted for expansion because "we need to get something before they leave," accelerating churn.

### 8. Cross-Sell Opportunities Are Mapped to Use Cases
- **Pass:** Cross-sell products are mapped to specific customer use cases and business outcomes. The recommendation is based on the customer's profile, not a generic catalog.
- **Fail:** Cross-sell is "let me tell you about our other products" without relevance to the customer's specific needs.

### 9. Expansion Impact on Customer Health Is Monitored
- **Pass:** After an expansion, the customer's health score is monitored for 90 days to ensure the expansion is being adopted and creating value (not sitting shelfware).
- **Fail:** Expansion revenue is booked and forgotten. Nobody checks whether the customer is actually using what they bought.

### 10. Expansion ROI for the Customer Is Quantifiable
- **Pass:** For each expansion sale, there is a projected ROI for the customer (time savings, revenue lift, risk reduction) that can be validated in a future QBR.
- **Fail:** Expansion is sold on features and potential rather than on projected, measurable customer ROI.
