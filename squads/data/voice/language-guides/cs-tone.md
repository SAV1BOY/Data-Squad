# Customer Success Tone Language Guide

## Purpose

This guide defines how the data team communicates with Customer Success teams and, through them, with customers. The goal is to translate data into customer health insights that CS can act on and, when needed, share directly with customers in language they understand.

## Core Principles

1. **Health-centered**: Frame metrics in terms of customer health, risk, and opportunity
2. **Actionable for CS**: Every data point should suggest a CS action (reach out, escalate, celebrate)
3. **Customer-safe language**: Assume anything you write may be forwarded to a customer
4. **Empathetic but factual**: Acknowledge the customer relationship context while staying grounded in data
5. **Timely**: Customer health signals degrade quickly; communicate urgency proportionally

## Communication Contexts

### Internal to CS Team
- Health score changes and risk alerts
- Usage trend summaries per account
- Renewal risk assessments
- Expansion opportunity signals
- Cohort comparisons for strategic accounts

### Customer-Facing (via CS)
- Business review data packages
- Usage reports for QBRs
- Benchmark comparisons
- ROI summaries

## Language Patterns

| Instead of | Use |
|---|---|
| "Churn probability is 0.73" | "This account shows several risk signals: login frequency dropped 60% and feature adoption is below the segment average" |
| "DAU is declining" | "The team's daily usage has decreased over the past three weeks, which often indicates a change in workflow or a potential blocker" |
| "NPS is 6" | "The customer's satisfaction score suggests they may have unresolved concerns worth a check-in" |
| "They are a low-value account" | "This account is in the Starter tier with usage patterns that suggest potential for growth" |
| "The data shows they will churn" | "Several leading indicators suggest this account may be at risk. Early outreach has historically improved outcomes in similar cases." |

## Formatting for CS Consumption

- Lead with the account name and health status
- Use a traffic light system: green (healthy), yellow (monitor), red (at risk)
- Include the specific metric that triggered the alert
- Suggest a CS action: check in, schedule review, escalate to manager
- Provide a one-sentence summary suitable for pasting into CRM notes

## Customer-Facing Data Guidelines

- Never expose internal scoring models or churn predictions to customers
- Present usage data as positive enablement: "Here is how your team is using the product"
- Frame benchmarks as opportunities: "Teams similar to yours tend to see X when they adopt Y"
- Avoid comparisons that make the customer feel behind: frame as "opportunity areas"
- Always pair a metric with context: what is normal, what is good, what the trend means

## Common Mistakes

- Sending raw data without context or suggested action to CS
- Using internal model names or score thresholds that mean nothing to CS
- Communicating churn risk without suggesting preventive actions
- Framing customer data negatively in materials that could be customer-facing
- Delivering health updates too late for CS to intervene
- Overloading CS with metrics when they need the top 2-3 signals
