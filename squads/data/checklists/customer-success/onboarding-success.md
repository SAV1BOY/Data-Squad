# CS Onboarding Success Checklist

## Purpose

Validate that customer success onboarding processes drive real product adoption, not just process completion. This gate ensures that new customers reach their first value milestone and that the onboarding experience is measured by outcomes, not by whether the CSM "finished the playbook."

---

## Checklist Items

### 1. Success Criteria Are Defined Per Customer Segment

- **Pass:** Each customer segment (enterprise, mid-market, SMB, self-serve) has a defined onboarding success milestone tied to product usage (e.g., "first integration live," "first report generated," "10 active users invited"). The milestone correlates with long-term retention.
- **Fail:** Onboarding success is defined as "kickoff call completed" or has no definition at all.

### 2. Time-to-Value Is Tracked and Benchmarked

- **Pass:** The time from contract signature (or sign-up) to the first value milestone is measured per segment. A benchmark exists (e.g., "median time-to-value for enterprise is 21 days"), and outliers beyond 2x the benchmark trigger escalation.
- **Fail:** No one measures how long onboarding takes, or "onboarding complete" is declared based on calendar time, not achievement.

### 3. Onboarding Completion Rate Is Monitored

- **Pass:** The percentage of new customers who reach the success milestone within the expected window is tracked monthly. The rate is segmented by CSM, customer size, and acquisition channel.
- **Fail:** The team knows how many customers started onboarding but not how many completed it.

### 4. Early Warning Signals Are Identified

- **Pass:** Leading indicators of onboarding failure (no login within 7 days, no data imported by Day 14, champion goes silent) are defined, tracked automatically, and trigger proactive CSM outreach.
- **Fail:** Struggling customers are only identified when they fail to renew months later.

### 5. Handoff From Sales to CS Is Clean

- **Pass:** A structured handoff document captures: customer goals, decision criteria, technical environment, key stakeholders, and any promises made during sales. The CSM reviews it before the kickoff call.
- **Fail:** The CSM starts from scratch, re-asking questions the customer already answered during sales, eroding trust.

### 6. Customer Effort During Onboarding Is Minimized

- **Pass:** The team measures customer effort (number of meetings required, hours of customer team time, number of support tickets during onboarding). Effort is reduced over time through better tooling, documentation, and automation.
- **Fail:** Onboarding requires 15 meetings and 40 hours of customer time, and no one tracks or attempts to reduce this.

### 7. Onboarding NPS or Satisfaction Is Captured

- **Pass:** A brief satisfaction survey is sent within one week of reaching the onboarding milestone. Scores below threshold trigger a follow-up conversation to address unresolved issues before they compound.
- **Fail:** No feedback is collected, or feedback is collected months later when the experience is no longer fresh.

### 8. Onboarding Outcomes Predict Renewal

- **Pass:** A retrospective analysis confirms that customers who complete onboarding successfully renew at a significantly higher rate than those who do not. This correlation is updated annually.
- **Fail:** The team believes onboarding matters but has never validated the link to renewal with data.
