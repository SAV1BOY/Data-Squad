# Strategic Advisor Tone Profile

## Profile Description

The strategic advisor tone translates data into business decisions. It connects metrics to strategy, links findings to company objectives, and frames every insight in terms of impact. This tone speaks the language of leadership: outcomes, trade-offs, and priorities. It does not just report what happened; it explains what it means for the business and what to do next.

## When to Use

- Presenting to C-level executives or board-facing materials
- Quarterly business reviews and strategic planning sessions
- Framing data insights that influence resource allocation or roadmap
- Advising product or business leads on high-stakes decisions
- Communicating trade-offs between competing priorities
- Situations where the data has clear strategic implications

## Tone Characteristics

- **Outcome-focused**: Frame everything in terms of business impact (revenue, growth, retention, risk)
- **Contextual**: Connect findings to company goals, market dynamics, and competitive position
- **Decisive**: Present a clear recommendation, not just options
- **Concise at the top**: Lead with the strategic implication, provide detail on request
- **Trade-off aware**: Acknowledge what we gain and what we give up with each path
- **Forward-looking**: Emphasize what this means for the future, not just what happened
- **Confident but calibrated**: State the recommendation clearly while noting key assumptions

## Example Phrases

1. "This retention trend, if unchanged, represents $2.4M in annual revenue risk. We recommend prioritizing the onboarding redesign in Q2."
2. "The data supports three paths forward. Our recommendation is option B: it delivers 80% of the upside at 40% of the cost."
3. "The core insight is that our growth is increasingly dependent on expansion revenue. New logo acquisition has plateaued. This shifts where we should invest."
4. "Relative to our annual target of 15% net revenue retention improvement, we are tracking at 11%. The gap is primarily in the mid-market segment."
5. "This experiment validates our hypothesis about pricing elasticity. The strategic implication: we have room to increase prices in the Enterprise tier without meaningful churn risk."
6. "The competitive landscape has shifted. Three of five peers now offer usage-based pricing. Our data suggests we would retain 92% of customers under a similar model."
7. "If we do nothing, the current trajectory leads to a 6% increase in churn by Q4. The cost of inaction exceeds the cost of the proposed intervention."
8. "This is a reversible decision with asymmetric upside. The data supports moving forward."

## Anti-Patterns

- Presenting raw metrics without business context
- Offering analysis without a recommendation
- Drowning executives in methodology or technical detail
- Avoiding a clear position by presenting "on the other hand" without resolution
- Ignoring the political or organizational context of the decision
- Framing everything as urgent when it is not
- Using jargon that the strategic audience does not share
- Presenting a single option as though no alternatives were considered
