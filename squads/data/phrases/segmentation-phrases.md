# Segmentation Phrases

## Purpose

Standardized phrases for describing, presenting, and interpreting user and account segments. Clear segmentation language prevents confusion about who is included, excluded, and why segments behave differently.

## Defining Segments

- "This segment includes [criteria]. It represents [X]% of [total population] ([Y] [users/accounts])."
- "We define [segment name] as [users/accounts] that meet all of the following criteria: [list]."
- "[Segment A] and [Segment B] are mutually exclusive: a [user/account] belongs to one or the other, not both."
- "This segmentation is based on [behavioral / demographic / firmographic / usage] data."
- "Segments are assigned at the [user / account / session] level, recalculated [daily / weekly / monthly]."
- "Users who do not meet any segment criteria are grouped as 'Unclassified' and represent [X]% of the population."

## Describing Segment Behavior

- "[Segment A] [metric] at [X]%, compared to [Y]% for [Segment B] — a difference of [Z] percentage points."
- "The highest-performing segment on [metric] is [segment], at [value]. The lowest is [segment], at [value]."
- "[Segment] shows a distinct pattern: [description of behavior], which differs from the overall population in [specific way]."
- "Behavior within [segment] is relatively homogeneous, with [X]% of members falling within [range] on [metric]."
- "There is significant variation within [segment]: the top quartile [metric] at [X], while the bottom quartile is at [Y]."
- "[Segment] accounts for [X]% of [total metric value] despite being only [Y]% of the population."

## Comparing Segments

- "Across all segments, [metric] ranges from [lowest value] ([segment]) to [highest value] ([segment])."
- "The gap between [Segment A] and [Segment B] on [metric] has [widened/narrowed] from [X] to [Y] over the past [period]."
- "When controlling for [variable], the difference between segments [persists/disappears], suggesting [interpretation]."
- "[Segment A] and [Segment B] share similar [attribute] but diverge on [metric], which points to [factor] as the differentiator."
- "Segment performance is not uniform: [X] segments are above target, [Y] are at target, and [Z] are below."

## Presenting Segment Insights

- "The overall [metric] of [X]% masks a critical difference between segments: [segment A] is at [Y]% and [segment B] is at [Z]%."
- "Breaking the data down by [dimension] reveals that the aggregate trend is driven almost entirely by [segment]."
- "Without segmentation, this looks like [misleading conclusion]. With segmentation, the real story is [accurate conclusion]."
- "Simpson's paradox is at play here: the overall trend goes [direction], but every individual segment goes [opposite direction], because [explanation]."
- "The segment-level view changes the recommendation: instead of [broad action], we should [targeted action] for [specific segment]."

## Segment Evolution

- "[Segment] grew by [X]% this [period], while [segment] declined by [Y]%."
- "Migration between segments: [X] [users/accounts] moved from [Segment A] to [Segment B] this [period]."
- "The composition of [segment] has shifted: [attribute] changed from [old distribution] to [new distribution]."
- "[Segment] is maturing: average tenure increased from [X] to [Y] months, and behavior is shifting toward [pattern]."
- "New [users/accounts] are increasingly entering [segment] rather than [segment], which changes our [metric] trajectory."

## Segment Limitations

- "This segmentation does not capture [specific nuance]. Treat boundaries as approximate."
- "Segments are based on [time period] data. Behavior may shift if the observation window changes."
- "Small segment sizes (n<[threshold]) reduce confidence in segment-level metrics. Interpret with caution."
- "The segments overlap on [dimension], which means [metric] attribution between segments is approximate."
- "Self-selection bias may affect segment comparisons: users in [segment] may differ in unmeasured ways."
