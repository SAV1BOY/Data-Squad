# Competing on Analytics - Thomas H. Davenport & Jeanne G. Harris (2007)

## Book Info

- **Title:** Competing on Analytics: The New Science of Winning
- **Authors:** Thomas H. Davenport, Jeanne G. Harris
- **Publisher:** Harvard Business Review Press
- **Year:** 2007 (updated edition 2017)
- **ISBN:** 978-1422103326
- **Pages:** 240
- **Context:** Davenport is a professor at Babson College and one of the most influential thinkers on knowledge management and analytics strategy. This book was one of the first to argue that analytics could be a primary source of competitive advantage, not just a support function. Published before "big data" became a buzzword, it framed analytics as a strategic capability.

## Core Thesis

Analytics is not just a tool for operational efficiency; it is a strategic weapon that can be the primary basis for competitive advantage. Companies like Capital One, Amazon, and Harrah's (Caesars) built their dominance not on better products but on superior analytical capabilities that informed every decision from customer acquisition to pricing to operations. Becoming an analytical competitor requires not just technology but a cultural commitment that starts at the CEO level and permeates the entire organization.

## Key Concepts

### 1. The Analytics Maturity Model (DELTA)
Five stages of analytical maturity: (a) **Analytically Impaired** -- data quality is poor, analysis is ad hoc. (b) **Localized Analytics** -- pockets of excellence exist but are not connected. (c) **Analytical Aspirations** -- leadership has committed but capabilities are still building. (d) **Analytical Companies** -- analytics informs most major decisions. (e) **Analytical Competitors** -- analytics is the primary basis for competitive advantage. The DELTA acronym: Data, Enterprise orientation, Leadership, Targets, Analysts.

### 2. DELTA Framework Components
- **Data:** Clean, integrated, accessible data is the foundation. Without data quality, analytics is building on sand.
- **Enterprise:** Analytics must be an enterprise-wide capability, not siloed in individual departments.
- **Leadership:** The CEO and senior leadership must champion analytics and model data-driven decision-making.
- **Targets:** Focus analytical investment on the business areas with the highest strategic payoff.
- **Analysts:** Hire, develop, and retain top analytical talent. The 10/90 rule applies here.

### 3. Strategic vs. Operational Analytics
Strategic analytics inform major decisions: which markets to enter, which customers to target, how to price products. Operational analytics optimize ongoing processes: supply chain, fraud detection, dynamic pricing. Most companies begin with operational analytics and graduate to strategic analytics as maturity increases.

### 4. The Analytical CEO
Organizations do not become analytical competitors without a CEO who personally values and uses data. Davenport profiles leaders like Jeff Bezos (Amazon), Gary Loveman (Harrah's), and Rich Fairbank (Capital One) who made analytical thinking central to their leadership. The CEO does not need to be a statistician but must insist on evidence-based decision-making.

### 5. Analytical Talent Management
The scarcest resource in analytics is not technology but people who combine technical skills, business acumen, and communication ability. Davenport identifies the "analytical professional" as someone who can move between data, models, and business implications. Retaining this talent requires meaningful work, career development, and organizational respect for the analytics function.

### 6. Embedding Analytics in Processes
Analytics creates value only when embedded in business processes and decision-making. A brilliant model that sits in a report nobody reads creates zero value. The goal is to make analytics an integral part of how decisions are made, not an afterthought. This requires changing workflows, decision rights, and incentive structures.

### 7. Analytics and Competitive Advantage
Analytics creates sustainable competitive advantage when it is: (a) difficult to replicate (requires proprietary data or unique analytical talent), (b) embedded in organizational processes (not just available but actually used), and (c) continuously improving (the capability compounds over time). Technology alone is easily copied; the combination of data, talent, and culture is not.

### 8. From Reporting to Optimization
The analytics value chain: reporting (what happened) leads to analysis (why it happened) leads to prediction (what will happen) leads to optimization (what should we do). Most organizations are stuck in reporting mode. Competitive advantage comes from moving up the chain to prediction and optimization.

## Application to Data Squad

- **Maturity assessment framework:** Use the DELTA model to assess client analytics maturity at the start of every engagement. This determines the appropriate scope and ambition of analytical initiatives.
- **Strategic focus:** Help clients identify the business domains where analytics will create the most strategic value rather than spreading analytical investment thinly across all functions.
- **Embedding analytics in workflows:** Design not just dashboards but the decision-making processes that use them. Specify who sees what data, what decisions they make, and how the decision loop closes.
- **Talent strategy:** Advise clients on analytical talent acquisition and development, using Davenport's framework for the skills and roles needed at each maturity stage.
- **CEO engagement:** Structure executive presentations to build leadership commitment to analytics by connecting data investments to competitive advantage stories.
- **From reporting to optimization roadmap:** Build a multi-year roadmap that moves clients from descriptive reporting through diagnostic analytics to predictive and prescriptive capabilities.

## Key Quotes

> "The companies that are most successful at analytics do not just use data to cut costs. They use it to create entirely new forms of competitive advantage."

> "You cannot buy your way to analytical competitiveness. Technology is necessary but not sufficient. You need data, talent, leadership, and a culture of evidence."

> "The biggest barrier to analytics is not technology. It is the willingness of leaders to make decisions based on data rather than intuition and politics."

> "Analytics creates sustainable competitive advantage because the combination of proprietary data, embedded processes, and skilled people is extraordinarily difficult to replicate."

> "Every company has data. Few companies have analytics. The difference is not tools; it is the decision to invest in the people and processes that turn data into action."
