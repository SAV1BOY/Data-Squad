# See-Think-Do-Care (STDC) Framework

> Source: Avinash Kaushik's See-Think-Do-Care business framework

---

## Overview

See-Think-Do-Care is a customer-intent framework that replaces the traditional sales
funnel with four audience clusters defined by commercial intent. Rather than pushing
customers through arbitrary funnel stages, STDC aligns content, channels, and
measurement to the intent a person actually has at a given moment. Each stage has its
own audience definition, content strategy, channel mix, and -- critically -- its own
success metrics.

---

## When to Use

- When the marketing team conflates awareness and acquisition metrics.
- When content strategy lacks a clear mapping to audience intent.
- When measurement plans treat all visitors as if they have purchase intent.
- When leadership asks why "traffic is up but conversions are flat."
- When building a full-funnel measurement plan for a new product or market.

---

## Core Concept

### The Four Intent Stages

| Stage    | Audience Definition                                         | Intent Level      |
|----------|-------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------|
| **See**  | The largest addressable qualified audience                  | No commercial intent |
| **Think**| Audience with some commercial intent (researching, comparing)| Weak to moderate  |
| **Do**   | Audience with strong commercial intent (ready to act)       | Strong            |
| **Care** | Existing customers with 2+ transactions                     | Loyalty / advocacy|

### Key Principles

1. **Intent defines the stage, not demographics.** The same person can be in different
   stages at different moments.
2. **Each stage deserves its own metrics.** Measuring "See" content by conversion rate
   is a category error.
3. **Content must match intent.** Sending "Do" content (buy now!) to a "See" audience
   destroys trust and wastes budget.
4. **Most organizations over-invest in "Do" and under-invest in "See" and "Care."**

## Steps / Process

### Step 1: Define Your Audience Clusters
- **See:** All people who *could* use your product category (not just your brand).
- **Think:** Subset of See who are actively researching or considering solutions.
- **Do:** Subset of Think who are ready to purchase, sign up, or convert.
- **Care:** Existing customers you want to retain and grow.

### Step 2: Map Content to Each Stage
| Stage | Content Types                                      |
|-------|----------------------------------------------------|
| See   | Brand storytelling, thought leadership, inspiration |
| Think | Comparison guides, how-tos, webinars, reviews      |
| Do    | Product pages, pricing, demos, promotions, CTAs     |
| Care  | Loyalty programs, community, exclusive content      |

### Step 3: Select Channels per Stage
| Stage | Primary Channels                                    |
|-------|-----------------------------------------------------|
| See   | Display, YouTube, social organic, PR, podcasts      |
| Think | Search (non-brand), social ads, email nurture, SEO  |
| Do    | Search (brand), retargeting, email offers, direct   |
| Care  | Email, in-app, community, customer success          |

### Step 4: Assign Metrics per Stage
| Stage | Primary Metrics                                            |
|-------|------------------------------------------------------------|
| See   | Reach, impressions, unaided awareness, new visitor %       |
| Think | Engagement rate, content consumption depth, email signups  |
| Do    | Conversion rate, revenue, CPA, ROAS, AOV                   |
| Care  | Repeat purchase rate, NPS, CLV, NRR, referral rate         |

### Step 5: Set Targets and Segments
- Apply the DMMM cascade (Objective -> Goal -> KPI -> Target -> Segment) within each
  stage to get actionable measurement.

### Step 6: Build Stage-Specific Dashboards
- Resist the urge to put all stages on one dashboard. Each stage has different
  consumers and different decision cadences.

## Pitfalls

1. **Measuring See content with Do metrics.** Brand awareness content should not be
   judged by conversion rate. This is the single most common mistake.

2. **Treating stages as sequential.** STDC is not a funnel. People can enter at any
   stage and move non-linearly.

3. **Ignoring the Care stage.** Most measurement plans stop at conversion. Existing
   customers are the highest-value segment and deserve dedicated metrics.

4. **Over-segmenting within stages.** Keep audience definitions broad enough to be
   useful. Micro-segments within "See" often lack statistical power.

5. **No feedback loops.** Insights from "Care" (what loyal customers value) should
   inform "See" content. Build cross-stage learning into your process.

6. **Conflating the framework with a media plan.** STDC is a *thinking* framework,
   not a media brief. It informs strategy; execution details come after.

7. **Static definitions.** As the market and product evolve, revisit audience
   definitions and stage boundaries quarterly.
