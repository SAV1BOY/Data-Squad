# Messaging to Metric Alignment

## Purpose

Ensure there is no disconnection between what the go-to-market message promises and what the metrics actually measure. Kao emphasizes that when messaging and metrics diverge, the team optimizes for numbers that do not reflect real customer value. This checklist audits the alignment chain from GTM messaging through to tracked outcomes.

---

## Checklist Items

### 1. The Core Value Proposition Is Written Down
- **Pass:** The product's core value proposition is documented in one sentence. Marketing, product, and sales teams can state it consistently.
- **Fail:** Different teams describe the product's value differently, or the value proposition has never been formally articulated.

### 2. Messaging Maps to a Measurable Customer Outcome
- **Pass:** For each key message (e.g., "Save 10 hours per week"), there is a corresponding metric that validates whether customers actually experience that outcome.
- **Fail:** Messaging makes promises ("grow faster," "work smarter") that are never measured on the customer side.

### 3. Acquisition Metrics Reflect Messaging Quality
- **Pass:** Acquisition metrics include quality indicators (activation rate of acquired users, time-to-value, first-session depth) not just volume, ensuring messaging attracts the right users.
- **Fail:** Acquisition is measured only by signup count, regardless of whether the message attracted users who will actually get value.

### 4. Landing Page Claims Are Testable
- **Pass:** Each quantitative claim on marketing pages (e.g., "3x faster," "used by 10,000 teams") is current, verifiable, and updated when the data changes.
- **Fail:** Claims are outdated, unverifiable, or based on cherry-picked data.

### 5. Sales Promises Are Tracked Post-Sale
- **Pass:** Commitments made during the sales process (features, outcomes, timelines) are logged and measured during onboarding and customer success reviews.
- **Fail:** Sales promises disappear after the contract is signed, creating a gap between expectation and delivery.

### 6. Feature Messaging Matches Feature Usage
- **Pass:** Features that are prominently marketed are also prominently adopted. If a marketed feature has low usage, this disconnect is flagged for investigation.
- **Fail:** The homepage highlights features that 90% of users never touch, indicating a messaging-reality gap.

### 7. Customer Feedback Validates Messaging
- **Pass:** Qualitative research (interviews, surveys, support conversations) confirms that customers describe the product's value in terms consistent with the messaging.
- **Fail:** Customers describe the value in terms the marketing team does not use, suggesting the messaging misses the actual value delivery.

### 8. Churn Reasons Are Compared to Messaging Gaps
- **Pass:** Exit surveys and churn analysis identify whether departing customers had unmet expectations traceable to messaging misalignment.
- **Fail:** Churn analysis does not examine whether the product failed to deliver on the promise that acquired the customer.

### 9. Metric Definitions Are Shared Across Teams
- **Pass:** Marketing, product, and customer success teams use the same metric definitions. "Active user," "engagement," and "success" mean the same thing everywhere.
- **Fail:** Each team has its own definition of core metrics, making cross-functional alignment impossible.

### 10. Messaging Updates Trigger Metric Reviews
- **Pass:** When messaging changes (new positioning, new value prop), the metrics team reviews whether existing metrics still capture the new promise, and adjusts tracking if needed.
- **Fail:** Messaging changes but metrics remain frozen, creating a growing disconnection over time.
