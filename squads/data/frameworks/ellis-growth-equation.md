# Ellis Growth Equation

## Overview

The Growth Equation is Sean Ellis's diagnostic framework that decomposes business growth into five
multiplicative components: Reach x Activation x Engagement x Revenue x Referral. Because the
components are multiplicative, a weakness in any single area constrains the entire system. The
framework enables growth teams to diagnose which factor is the weakest link and focus effort
accordingly, rather than spreading thin across all dimensions or defaulting to the most visible
one (usually reach).

## Origin

Developed by **Sean Ellis** and refined through his work with dozens of high-growth startups
including Dropbox, LogMeIn, and Eventbrite, and subsequently codified in *Hacking Growth* (2017),
co-authored with Morgan Brown. The equation formalizes the growth team's diagnostic process: instead
of asking "how do we grow?" (which invites unfocused brainstorming), it asks "which growth lever is
currently the most constrained?" The multiplicative structure was a deliberate design choice to
emphasize that a zero or near-zero in any component nullifies the others.

## Core Model

### Growth = Reach x Activation x Engagement x Revenue x Referral

### Component 1: Reach
The number of people who encounter your product or brand.

**Metrics:**
- Website unique visitors
- App store impressions
- Social media reach and impressions
- Search impressions for target queries
- Referral traffic volume
- Paid channel reach
- PR and earned media exposure

**Healthy indicators:** Growing reach from diversified channels, improving organic-to-paid ratio,
reach within target audience segments.

**Weakness signals:** Flat or declining unique visitors, over-reliance on single channel, high
reach but low brand awareness in target market.

### Component 2: Activation
The percentage of reached users who experience the core value of the product.

**Metrics:**
- Sign-up or registration rate
- Onboarding completion rate
- Time to first value-generating action
- "Aha moment" completion rate
- First-session engagement depth
- Setup completion rate

**Healthy indicators:** Rising activation rates over time, decreasing time-to-value, most users
reaching the aha moment within first session.

**Weakness signals:** High sign-up volume with low activation, long time-to-value, users
abandoning onboarding flows, low aha moment completion.

### Component 3: Engagement
The depth and frequency of product usage among activated users.

**Metrics:**
- Daily/weekly/monthly active users (DAU/WAU/MAU)
- DAU/MAU ratio (stickiness)
- Feature adoption breadth and depth
- Session frequency and duration
- Content consumption patterns
- Core action frequency (posts, uploads, searches, whatever drives value)

**Healthy indicators:** High DAU/MAU ratio, increasing feature adoption, growing session frequency,
strong usage habit formation.

**Weakness signals:** Declining DAU/MAU, feature adoption plateauing, usage concentrated in
single feature, declining session frequency after initial spike.

### Component 4: Revenue
The monetization of engaged users.

**Metrics:**
- Conversion rate from free to paid
- Average revenue per user (ARPU)
- Average revenue per paying user (ARPPU)
- Revenue per session or visit
- Upgrade and expansion rate
- Pricing page conversion rate
- Cart/checkout completion rate

**Healthy indicators:** Growing ARPU, healthy free-to-paid conversion, increasing expansion
revenue, pricing power.

**Weakness signals:** Declining ARPU, low free-to-paid conversion despite high engagement,
price sensitivity, heavy reliance on discounting.

### Component 5: Referral
The degree to which existing users bring new users.

**Metrics:**
- Viral coefficient (K-factor): invites sent x conversion rate of invites
- Referral program participation rate
- Net Promoter Score as a proxy for referral propensity
- Organic word-of-mouth (measured through branded search, direct traffic)
- Social sharing rate
- User-generated content volume

**Healthy indicators:** K-factor approaching or exceeding 1.0, growing branded search, strong NPS,
active referral program participation.

**Weakness signals:** K-factor well below 1.0, no organic word-of-mouth, low NPS, referral program
exists but unused.

### The Multiplicative Effect

Because the components multiply, improvements compound:
- Improving Reach by 20% AND Activation by 20% yields 44% growth (1.2 x 1.2 = 1.44)
- But a 50% deficiency in Activation limits the entire system regardless of Reach improvement

This structure makes the diagnosis clear: find the smallest multiplier and improve it. A 20%
improvement in the weakest link often delivers more growth than a 50% improvement in the strongest.

## Application Steps

### Step 1: Measure Each Component
Establish baseline metrics for all five components. Use the metrics listed above as starting points,
selecting those most relevant to your business model. Ensure measurement is consistent and reliable.

### Step 2: Benchmark Against Reasonable Standards
Compare each component to industry benchmarks, competitor data (where available), or internal
historical performance. Identify which components are below expectations.

### Step 3: Identify the Weakest Link
Which component, if improved, would have the greatest impact on overall growth? Often this is
intuitive once the data is assembled. If Activation is 15% and Engagement DAU/MAU is 35%, the
activation bottleneck is likely the binding constraint.

### Step 4: Diagnose the Root Cause
Why is the weakest component weak? Drill into sub-metrics. If Activation is the weakest link:
- Is sign-up the bottleneck? (Reach to registration)
- Is onboarding the bottleneck? (Registration to first value action)
- Is the aha moment unclear or hard to reach?

### Step 5: Generate Hypotheses
Brainstorm specific interventions that could improve the weakest component. Use qualitative data
(user interviews, session recordings, support tickets) to inform hypotheses.

### Step 6: Test and Iterate
Use the high-tempo testing framework to rapidly test hypotheses. Measure impact on the specific
component metric. When one component improves sufficiently, re-assess to identify the new
weakest link.

### Step 7: Re-Diagnose Regularly
The weakest link changes as improvements are made. After fixing activation, engagement may become
the constraint. The growth equation is a continuous diagnostic cycle, not a one-time assessment.

## Key Distinctions

- **Growth is a system, not a channel.** The equation frames growth as an interconnected system
  where all components must function. Optimizing one component in isolation is insufficient.
- **The weakest link determines system performance.** In a multiplicative system, the smallest
  factor is the bottleneck. This focuses effort where it matters most.
- **Referral is a component, not a strategy.** Referral programs only work when the other four
  components are healthy. Users do not refer products they are not engaged with.
- **Revenue follows engagement.** Monetization efforts on unengaged users are premature. The
  equation's ordering reflects a natural dependency chain.
- **The equation assumes PMF.** Without product-market fit, the entire equation is academic.
  PMF is the prerequisite that makes growth investment rational.

## Pitfalls

1. **Optimizing reach without activation.** Pouring money into acquisition when activation is
   broken is the most common growth mistake. It fills the top of a leaky bucket.
2. **Measuring components in isolation.** Each component affects the others. Improving activation
   quality may change engagement patterns. Treat the equation as a system.
3. **Ignoring referral.** Referral is often the last component teams address, but it is the only
   one that creates compounding organic growth without incremental cost.
4. **Confusing activity with progress.** Running experiments across all five components
   simultaneously may feel productive but dilutes focus. Concentrate on the binding constraint.
5. **Using vanity metrics within components.** Total sign-ups (reach) without quality filtering
   inflates the appearance of the reach component. Quality metrics within each component matter.
6. **Forgetting that the equation changes.** What is true at $1M ARR is not true at $50M ARR.
   The binding constraint shifts as the business scales. Regular reassessment is essential.
7. **Treating the equation as linear.** Users do not flow neatly from Reach to Referral. The
   equation is a measurement framework, not a user journey map.
