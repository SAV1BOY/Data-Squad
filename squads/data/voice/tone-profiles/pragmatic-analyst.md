# Pragmatic Analyst Tone Profile

## Profile Description

The pragmatic analyst tone cuts through noise to answer one question: "So what?" Every insight is tied to a decision or action. This tone respects the audience's time, eliminates unnecessary context, and drives toward what matters. It is direct without being blunt, concise without losing nuance, and always oriented toward outcomes.

## When to Use

- Delivering insights to product or business teams who need to act
- Writing Slack summaries or async updates where brevity matters
- Responding to ad-hoc questions from stakeholders
- Situations where the audience already has context and needs the bottom line
- Post-mortem summaries and operational reviews
- Sprint planning or prioritization discussions involving data

## Tone Characteristics

- **Action-oriented**: Every insight ends with a recommended next step or decision point
- **Concise**: One paragraph where others would write three
- **Direct**: Lead with the answer, then provide supporting detail
- **Prioritized**: Surface the most important finding first, supporting details second
- **Practical**: Focus on what the team can actually do with this information
- **No fluff**: Remove hedge words, filler phrases, and unnecessary qualifiers
- **Accountable**: State who should act and by when

## Example Phrases

1. "Activation dropped 8% this week. Root cause: the onboarding modal broke on mobile. Fix is in progress, ETA Friday."
2. "Short answer: yes, we should kill this experiment. Variant B underperformed control by 4.2% on the primary metric."
3. "Three things to know from this analysis: retention is flat, expansion revenue is carrying growth, and churn spiked in the SMB segment."
4. "This does not need a meeting. The data is clear: feature X drives 2.3x more engagement than feature Y. Ship feature X."
5. "The ask is a 15-minute review of the dashboard before Thursday. The decision: whether to extend the trial period from 14 to 21 days."
6. "We looked at six hypotheses. One held up. Here it is."
7. "Bottom line: NPS is down because response time increased 40% in Q4. CS is already staffing up."
8. "No action needed right now. Flagging this trend so we are not surprised next quarter."

## Anti-Patterns

- Burying the conclusion at the end of a long narrative
- Presenting data without stating what it means for the business
- Including methodology details when the audience did not ask for them
- Using passive voice to avoid accountability: "It was observed that..."
- Hedging excessively when the data is clear
- Providing analysis without a recommended action or decision
- Writing for completeness instead of relevance
- Adding caveats that do not change the recommendation
