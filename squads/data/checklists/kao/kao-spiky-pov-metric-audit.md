# Kao Spiky POV Metric Audit

## Purpose

Audit whether the metrics and KPIs tell a differentiated story -- a "spiky point of view." Kao argues that generic metrics produce generic strategies. The best growth teams choose metrics that reflect their unique product thesis, competitive positioning, and customer value proposition. This checklist tests whether your metrics have a point of view or are just industry defaults.

---

## Checklist Items

### 1. At Least One Metric Is Unique to This Product
- **Pass:** The metric set includes at least one KPI that is specific to this product's value proposition and would not appear on a competitor's dashboard (e.g., "collaborative sessions per team per week" for a collaboration tool).
- **Fail:** Every metric is a generic industry standard (DAU, MAU, MRR, NPS) with nothing that reflects the specific value this product delivers.

### 2. The Unique Metric Captures the Core Value Exchange
- **Pass:** The differentiated metric directly measures the moment of value delivery -- the thing that makes this product worth using. If this metric goes up, customers are genuinely better off.
- **Fail:** The unique metric is a novelty metric that sounds interesting but does not connect to customer value.

### 3. The Metric Reflects the Product's Thesis
- **Pass:** The metric embodies the product team's belief about why this product wins (e.g., "We win because teams that collaborate in real-time ship faster -- so we measure real-time co-editing sessions").
- **Fail:** The metric set does not reflect any product thesis. It could belong to any product in the category.

### 4. The Metric Differentiates from Competitors
- **Pass:** If a competitor optimized for generic engagement metrics while you optimize for your spiky metric, you would build a meaningfully different product. The metric creates strategic divergence.
- **Fail:** Optimizing for your metrics would lead to the same product decisions as optimizing for any competitor's metrics.

### 5. The Metric Is Defensible Under Scrutiny
- **Pass:** The team can explain to a skeptical board member or investor why this metric matters more than industry-standard alternatives. The explanation is evidence-backed.
- **Fail:** The metric sounds clever but crumbles under questioning. It is differentiated for differentiation's sake.

### 6. The Metric Has Predictive Power
- **Pass:** The spiky metric correlates with downstream business outcomes (retention, expansion, revenue). Teams that score high on this metric have better business outcomes.
- **Fail:** The metric is interesting but does not predict anything useful about business health.

### 7. The Metric Influences Product Decisions
- **Pass:** Product roadmap discussions reference the spiky metric. Features are evaluated based on whether they will move it. It shapes build vs. not-build decisions.
- **Fail:** The metric exists on a dashboard but does not appear in product planning conversations.

### 8. The Metric Is Explainable to Customers
- **Pass:** You could share this metric with a customer, and they would recognize it as meaningful ("Yes, that is what I value about your product"). It resonates with their experience.
- **Fail:** The metric is internally meaningful but would confuse or bore a customer.

### 9. The Metric Set Avoids "Metric Theater"
- **Pass:** Every metric in the set exists to inform a decision. There are no metrics kept solely because they look good in investor updates or press releases.
- **Fail:** Metrics are selected partly for their narrative appeal to external audiences rather than for internal decision-making utility.

### 10. The Spiky POV Is Revisited as the Product Evolves
- **Pass:** As the product matures and the value proposition evolves, the spiky metric is re-evaluated. What differentiated the product at launch may not differentiate it at scale.
- **Fail:** The spiky metric was chosen early and never revisited, even though the product has evolved significantly.
