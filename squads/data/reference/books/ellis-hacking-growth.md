# Hacking Growth - Sean Ellis & Morgan Brown (2017)

## Book Info

- **Title:** Hacking Growth: How Today's Fastest-Growing Companies Drive Breakout Success
- **Authors:** Sean Ellis, Morgan Brown
- **Publisher:** Currency / Crown Business
- **Year:** 2017
- **ISBN:** 978-0451497215
- **Pages:** 320
- **Context:** Sean Ellis coined the term "growth hacking" in 2010 and ran early growth at Dropbox, LogMeIn, and Eventbrite. Morgan Brown is a growth practitioner and co-founder of GrowthHackers.com. The book codifies the growth team process that Ellis developed and that was adopted by companies from Facebook to Airbnb.

## Core Thesis

Sustainable, rapid growth is not the result of a single silver bullet or viral hack. It is the product of a systematic, cross-functional process of continuous experimentation across the entire customer journey -- acquisition, activation, retention, revenue, and referral. Growth teams combine marketing, product, engineering, and data analysis to generate high-velocity hypotheses, prioritize them rigorously, test them rapidly, and double down on what works. The process, not any individual tactic, is the competitive advantage.

## Key Concepts

### 1. The Growth Team Structure
A cross-functional team with a growth lead, product manager, engineers, data analyst, and marketers. The team operates with its own sprint cadence (typically weekly), maintains its own backlog of experiments, and has authority to modify both the product and the marketing funnel. Key distinction: growth is not a marketing function; it sits at the intersection of product and marketing.

### 2. The North Star Metric
Every growth team must identify a single North Star Metric that captures the core value the product delivers to customers. For Airbnb: nights booked. For Facebook: daily active users. For Slack: messages sent. The North Star aligns all growth experiments toward value creation rather than vanity metrics. It must be: measurable, actionable, and directly correlated with customer value and business revenue.

### 3. The Growth Hacking Process (GHPP)
A four-phase loop: (a) **Analyze** -- dive deep into data to understand the current funnel, identify drop-off points, and surface opportunities. (b) **Ideate** -- generate experiment hypotheses from the entire team. (c) **Prioritize** -- use ICE scoring (Impact, Confidence, Ease) or similar frameworks to rank experiments. (d) **Test** -- execute the highest-priority experiments in rapid cycles. Repeat weekly.

### 4. Aha Moment and Activation
The "aha moment" is the experience where a new user first realizes the product's core value. Growth teams must identify this moment through data analysis (correlating early behaviors with long-term retention), then engineer the onboarding flow to get users there as fast as possible. Activation is the most underleveraged growth lever.

### 5. Retention is the Foundation
No amount of acquisition can compensate for a leaky bucket. Ellis and Brown argue that retention must be addressed before scaling acquisition. They distinguish initial retention (first days/weeks), medium-term retention (weeks/months), and long-term retention (ongoing engagement), each requiring different strategies.

### 6. ICE Scoring Framework
A lightweight prioritization method for growth experiments: Impact (how much will this move the metric if it works?), Confidence (how sure are we it will work, based on data or precedent?), and Ease (how quickly and cheaply can we run the test?). Each dimension is scored 1-10 and averaged. High-ICE experiments run first.

### 7. High-Tempo Testing
The growth team's velocity is measured in experiments per week. Ellis advocates a minimum cadence of 2-3 experiments per week at scale. High velocity creates a compounding advantage: more tests mean more learning, more learning means better hypotheses, better hypotheses mean higher win rates.

### 8. Viral Loops and Network Effects
Growth teams engineer referral mechanics into the product: invite flows, share incentives, viral content loops, and network-effect features. The viral coefficient (K-factor) and viral cycle time are key metrics. Even a K-factor below 1.0 meaningfully reduces acquisition costs.

## Application to Data Squad

- **Growth audit framework:** Use the AARRR funnel (pirate metrics) combined with Ellis's growth process to audit where clients have the biggest leaky bucket. Most companies over-invest in acquisition and under-invest in activation and retention.
- **North Star Metric definition:** Help clients define their North Star Metric early in any engagement. This single decision aligns all subsequent analytics and experimentation work.
- **Experiment velocity tracking:** Measure and report on the client's experiment velocity (tests per week) as a meta-metric. Companies that test more, learn faster.
- **ICE scoring for analytics roadmaps:** Apply ICE scoring to prioritize analytics and data science projects, not just growth experiments. Data Squad's own project backlog benefits from this discipline.
- **Activation analysis:** Build "aha moment" analyses as a standard deliverable: correlate early user behaviors with 30/60/90-day retention to identify the activation behaviors worth optimizing.
- **Retention curves as baseline:** Before recommending any growth initiatives, establish retention curves by cohort. If retention is poor, prioritize fixing it before scaling acquisition.

## Key Quotes

> "Growth hacking is not about finding a silver bullet. It is about building a machine that generates, tests, and learns from hundreds of small experiments."

> "The aha moment is the foundation of all growth. If users don't experience your core value quickly, nothing else matters."

> "Retention is the king of growth. A product with great retention and mediocre acquisition will always beat a product with great acquisition and mediocre retention."

> "The best growth teams are not the ones with the best ideas. They are the ones that test the most ideas."
