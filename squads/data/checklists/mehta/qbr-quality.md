# QBR Quality

## Purpose

Audit the Quarterly Business Review process for storytelling, metric rigor, action orientation, and customer impact. Mehta emphasizes that QBRs should be strategic conversations about customer outcomes and mutual value, not slide-reading exercises about feature usage.

---

## Checklist Items

### 1. The QBR Tells a Story, Not Just Reports Numbers
- **Pass:** The QBR follows a narrative arc: where the customer started, what was accomplished, what results were achieved, and where to go next. Data supports the story rather than replacing it.
- **Fail:** The QBR is a series of charts with no narrative thread. The customer sees numbers but no story about their journey.

### 2. Customer Outcomes Are the Headline
- **Pass:** The QBR leads with business outcomes the customer has achieved (cost savings, revenue growth, efficiency gains) rather than product usage statistics.
- **Fail:** The QBR leads with feature adoption stats and login counts, which may not connect to what the customer actually cares about.

### 3. Metrics Are Relevant to the Customer's Goals
- **Pass:** The metrics presented map to goals established at the start of the engagement (or in the previous QBR). Each metric answers "are we making progress toward what you hired us to do?"
- **Fail:** Metrics are generic product usage numbers that do not connect to the customer's stated objectives.

### 4. Risks Are Surfaced Honestly
- **Pass:** The QBR includes a section on risks, challenges, and areas of concern. Problems are named openly rather than hidden behind good-news charts.
- **Fail:** The QBR is entirely positive, creating a credibility gap when the customer is aware of issues that go unmentioned.

### 5. A Forward-Looking Plan Exists
- **Pass:** The QBR includes a plan for the next quarter with specific goals, milestones, and owners on both the vendor and customer side.
- **Fail:** The QBR looks only backward. There is no plan for what comes next.

### 6. Action Items Are Captured and Tracked
- **Pass:** Every QBR produces a documented list of action items with owners and deadlines. Action items from the previous QBR are reviewed for completion.
- **Fail:** Action items are discussed but not written down, or previous action items are never revisited.

### 7. The Right People Are in the Room
- **Pass:** QBR attendees include the customer's decision-maker (or economic buyer), the day-to-day champion, and the vendor's CSM and relevant specialists. Attendee planning happens before the meeting.
- **Fail:** QBRs happen with only operational contacts, missing the decision-maker who controls renewal and expansion.

### 8. QBR Content Is Customized
- **Pass:** Each QBR is customized for the specific customer. Templates are used for structure but content reflects this customer's unique goals, industry, and journey.
- **Fail:** All customers receive the same generic QBR with only the company name changed.

### 9. The QBR Influences Renewal and Expansion
- **Pass:** QBR outcomes (demonstrated value, identified gaps, forward plan) directly feed into renewal and expansion conversations. The QBR is a strategic tool, not a compliance exercise.
- **Fail:** QBRs happen on a schedule but have no connection to commercial outcomes. Renewal conversations happen separately with no QBR input.

### 10. QBR Cadence Matches Customer Importance
- **Pass:** High-value customers receive full quarterly QBRs. Mid-tier customers receive lighter-touch reviews (possibly digital). Low-tier customers receive automated value summaries.
- **Fail:** All customers receive the same QBR cadence regardless of value, or QBRs are skipped for top-tier accounts due to CSM workload.
