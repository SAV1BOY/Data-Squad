# Inspired - Marty Cagan (2017, 2nd Edition)

## Book Info

- **Title:** Inspired: How to Create Tech Products Customers Love
- **Author:** Marty Cagan
- **Publisher:** Wiley
- **Year:** 2017 (2nd edition; 1st edition 2008)
- **ISBN:** 978-1119387503
- **Pages:** 368
- **Context:** Marty Cagan is the founder of the Silicon Valley Product Group (SVPG) and former VP of Product at eBay, Netscape, and HP. Inspired is widely considered the definitive book on modern product management. The 2nd edition substantially updated the framework for the era of continuous discovery and delivery.

## Core Thesis

The best tech products are built by empowered product teams that are given problems to solve, not features to build. These teams use a rigorous product discovery process to identify solutions that are valuable (customers want it), usable (customers can figure it out), feasible (engineers can build it), and viable (it works for the business). Data is central to every phase: identifying opportunities, validating solutions, and measuring outcomes. The biggest product failures come from teams that skip discovery and jump straight to delivery.

## Key Concepts

### 1. Product Discovery vs. Product Delivery
Discovery is the process of figuring out what to build. Delivery is the process of building it. Most companies spend 95% of their effort on delivery and 5% on discovery, which is exactly backwards. Discovery should generate validated evidence that a solution is worth building before engineering resources are committed.

### 2. The Four Risks
Every product idea carries four risks: (a) **Value risk** -- will customers buy or choose to use it? (b) **Usability risk** -- can customers figure out how to use it? (c) **Feasibility risk** -- can we build it with the time, skills, and technology available? (d) **Business viability risk** -- does it work for the business (legal, financial, brand)? Product discovery systematically addresses all four.

### 3. Empowered Product Teams
A cross-functional team with a product manager, product designer, and tech lead (plus engineers) that is given a business problem or objective, not a feature roadmap. The team owns both the problem and the solution. They are measured by outcomes (business results), not outputs (features shipped).

### 4. Opportunity Assessment
Before working on any idea, answer four questions: What business objective does this address? How will we measure success? What does success look like for the customer? What is the opportunity size? This assessment prevents building solutions in search of problems.

### 5. Product Vision and Strategy
The product vision describes where you want to be in 2-5 years. The product strategy is the sequence of target markets, personas, and business outcomes that will get you there. OKRs (Objectives and Key Results) translate strategy into quarterly team-level objectives. Data informs strategy through market sizing, customer segmentation, and competitive analysis.

### 6. Continuous Discovery
Discovery is not a phase; it is a continuous practice running in parallel with delivery. Techniques include: customer interviews, prototype testing, A/B experiments, data mining, feasibility spikes, and business case analysis. The goal is to generate evidence quickly and cheaply before committing to expensive engineering.

### 7. Quantitative and Qualitative Evidence
Good product decisions require both quantitative data (usage analytics, funnel metrics, A/B test results) and qualitative understanding (customer interviews, usability tests, contextual inquiry). Relying exclusively on either one leads to blind spots. Quantitative data tells you what is happening; qualitative research tells you why.

### 8. Outcome-Based Roadmaps
Replace feature-based roadmaps (lists of features with dates) with outcome-based roadmaps (business outcomes the team will pursue, with the team empowered to discover the best solutions). This requires clear metrics for each outcome and a culture of accountability for results.

## Application to Data Squad

- **Product analytics design:** Help clients instrument their products to generate the quantitative evidence Cagan's discovery process requires. Event tracking, funnel analytics, and feature adoption metrics are the foundation.
- **Outcome metrics for product teams:** Work with product teams to define the outcome metrics for their OKRs. These must be measurable, attributable, and connected to business value.
- **Discovery data support:** Provide analytical support during product discovery: market sizing, opportunity scoring, prototype test analysis, and competitive benchmarking.
- **A/B test infrastructure:** Build the experimentation infrastructure that empowered product teams need for continuous discovery.
- **Roadmap metrics:** Help product leaders transition from feature-based to outcome-based roadmaps by defining the metrics that will measure each objective.
- **Qualitative-quantitative synthesis:** Design research programs that pair quantitative product analytics with qualitative customer research, ensuring product teams have both halves of the evidence equation.
