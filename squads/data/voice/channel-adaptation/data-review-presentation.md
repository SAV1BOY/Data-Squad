# Data Review Presentation Guide

## Purpose

This guide defines how to structure and deliver data review presentations, whether for weekly squad reviews, monthly business reviews, or ad-hoc deep dives. The goal is to make presentations efficient, insightful, and decision-oriented.

## Core Principles

1. **Decide before you present**: Know the one thing you want the audience to take away
2. **Respect time**: A 30-minute slot means 15 minutes of presentation and 15 minutes of discussion
3. **Visual clarity**: Every slide should be readable in 5 seconds
4. **Progressive depth**: Start with the conclusion, drill into detail only as needed
5. **End with a decision**: Every review should close with a decision point or agreed action

## Presentation Structure

### Opening (2 minutes)
- State the purpose of the review in one sentence
- Set expectations: what you will cover and what you will not
- Preview the key finding or recommendation

### Context (3 minutes)
- Relevant time period and scope
- Any changes since the last review (methodology, data source, definitions)
- Business context that affects interpretation

### Key Findings (8-10 minutes)
- Present findings in order of importance, not chronological order
- Each finding follows the pattern: observation, evidence, implication
- Use 1 chart per finding; annotate the takeaway directly on the chart
- Limit to 3-5 key findings; put the rest in the appendix

### Recommendation (2 minutes)
- State the recommended action clearly
- Quantify the expected impact
- Name the trade-offs or risks
- Identify who owns the next step

### Discussion (remaining time)
- Facilitate, do not defend
- Have backup slides ready for anticipated questions
- Capture decisions and action items in real time

## Slide Design Standards

- **Title**: Every slide title is a complete sentence that states the finding (not "Retention Analysis" but "Retention dropped 3 points, concentrated in paid search")
- **One chart per slide**: If you need two charts, use two slides
- **Annotate charts**: Circle, arrow, or callout pointing to the key insight
- **Minimize text**: If you need a paragraph, it belongs in a document, not a slide
- **Consistent axes**: Use the same scale when comparing charts across slides
- **Source line**: Bottom of every chart: data source, date range, sample size

## Facilitation Techniques

- Start with: "The one thing to take away from today is..."
- Pause after key findings for questions before moving on
- If discussion goes deep on one topic: "Let us take this offline and cover the remaining findings"
- End with: "The decision we need is... and the owner is..."
- Send a follow-up message within 24 hours with decisions and action items

## Common Mistakes

- Presenting 25 slides in 30 minutes and skipping discussion entirely
- Using slide titles that describe the chart type instead of the finding
- Including charts without annotations, leaving interpretation to the audience
- Presenting findings without a recommendation
- Not having an appendix ready for deep-dive questions
- Reading the slides aloud instead of narrating the insight
- Ending without a clear decision or action item

## Review Types and Adaptations

| Review Type | Frequency | Audience Level | Depth | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Weekly squad review | Weekly | Analyst-PM | Operational | 30 min |
| Monthly business review | Monthly | Director-VP | Strategic | 45-60 min |
| Quarterly business review | Quarterly | C-level | Executive | 30 min |
| Deep dive | Ad hoc | Analyst | Technical | 45-60 min |
| Post-mortem | As needed | Mixed | Root cause | 30-45 min |
