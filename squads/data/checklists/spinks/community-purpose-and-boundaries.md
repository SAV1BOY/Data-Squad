# Community Purpose and Boundaries

## Purpose

Audit whether the community has a clearly articulated purpose, defined rules, and explicit boundaries on what is not permitted. David Spinks emphasizes that communities without clear purpose drift into noise, and communities without boundaries become toxic. This checklist ensures the foundation is solid.

---

## Checklist Items

### 1. The Community Purpose Is Written in One Sentence
- **Pass:** The community has a single-sentence purpose statement that answers: "This community exists so that [audience] can [achieve outcome]." It is visible in the community's about page and onboarding.
- **Fail:** The purpose is vague ("connect with others"), missing entirely, or requires a paragraph to explain.

### 2. The Purpose Serves Members, Not Just the Brand
- **Pass:** The purpose statement centers on member value (learning, support, connection, career growth) rather than company goals (lead generation, product feedback, reducing support costs).
- **Fail:** The community exists primarily as a marketing channel disguised as a community. Members can feel the extraction.

### 3. Community Rules Are Published and Accessible
- **Pass:** A clear, concise set of community guidelines is published, linked from the onboarding flow, and easily findable. Rules cover expected behavior, not just prohibited behavior.
- **Fail:** No written rules exist, or they are buried in a terms-of-service document that nobody reads.

### 4. What Is NOT Permitted Is Explicit
- **Pass:** The guidelines explicitly state what is not allowed: spam, self-promotion beyond defined limits, harassment, misinformation, off-topic content. Each prohibition includes consequences.
- **Fail:** Rules are all positive ("be kind, be helpful") without addressing what happens when someone breaks them.

### 5. Enforcement Is Consistent
- **Pass:** Rule violations are addressed consistently regardless of who the violator is (including high-status members and company employees). Enforcement actions are documented.
- **Fail:** Rules exist but are enforced selectively, creating a perception of favoritism or lawlessness.

### 6. Consequences Are Graduated
- **Pass:** There is a graduated response system: warning, temporary restriction, permanent ban. Escalation criteria are documented. Members know what to expect.
- **Fail:** The only options are "ignore it" or "ban," with nothing in between.

### 7. Scope Is Defined
- **Pass:** The community has defined topic boundaries: what is on-topic and what is not. Off-topic conversations are redirected rather than ignored.
- **Fail:** The community tries to be about everything, resulting in unfocused conversations and declining signal-to-noise ratio.

### 8. The Purpose Aligns with the Business Model
- **Pass:** There is a documented connection between the community's purpose and the company's business goals. Both sides benefit: members get value, and the company gets a measurable outcome.
- **Fail:** The community exists because "every company should have one" with no articulation of how it serves the business.

### 9. Members Were Consulted on Purpose and Rules
- **Pass:** Community guidelines were developed with input from founding members or early participants. Member feedback on rules is collected and incorporated.
- **Fail:** Rules were written by the community team in isolation with no member input.

### 10. Purpose and Rules Are Reviewed Annually
- **Pass:** The purpose statement and community guidelines are reviewed at least annually, with updates reflecting how the community has evolved.
- **Fail:** Purpose and rules were set at launch and have not been revisited despite significant growth or change in the community.
