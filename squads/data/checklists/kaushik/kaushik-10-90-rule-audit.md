# Kaushik 10/90 Rule Audit

## Purpose

Avinash Kaushik's 10/90 rule states that for every $100 invested in analytics, $10 should go to tools and $90 to the people who interpret data and drive action. This checklist audits whether the organization's analytics investment is balanced, or whether it suffers from the common pathology of expensive tools with nobody skilled enough to use them.

---

## Checklist Items

### 1. Tool Spend vs. People Spend Ratio Is Known
- **Pass:** The organization can state its analytics tool budget and its analytics people budget (salaries, training, contractors). The ratio is documented.
- **Fail:** Nobody has calculated the ratio. Tool costs are known but people costs are not tracked against analytics specifically.

### 2. The Ratio Trends Toward 10/90
- **Pass:** People investment is at least 5x tool investment. If the ratio is currently 50/50 or worse, there is a documented plan to shift it.
- **Fail:** The ratio is 70/30 (tools over people) or worse, and there is no plan to rebalance.

### 3. Analysts Have Time for Analysis, Not Just Reporting
- **Pass:** Analysts spend at least 50% of their time on exploratory analysis, hypothesis testing, and insight generation -- not on pulling and formatting reports.
- **Fail:** Analysts are full-time report generators with no time for thinking.

### 4. Training Budget Exists and Is Used
- **Pass:** There is an annual training budget for analytics staff covering courses, conferences, and certifications. Budget utilization is above 70%.
- **Fail:** No training budget exists, or it exists on paper but is never spent because "we're too busy."

### 5. Analysis Outputs Reach Decision-Makers
- **Pass:** Analysis work products (not just dashboards, but actual analysis decks with recommendations) are regularly presented to and discussed by leadership.
- **Fail:** Analysis sits in shared drives unread, or analysts present to other analysts but never to decision-makers.

### 6. Tool Capabilities Are Actually Utilized
- **Pass:** An audit of the analytics tool suite shows that at least 60% of purchased features/modules are actively used.
- **Fail:** The organization pays for an enterprise analytics suite but uses it as a glorified hit counter.

### 7. Tool Consolidation Has Been Evaluated
- **Pass:** The tool stack has been reviewed in the last 12 months for overlap, redundancy, and cost optimization. Unused tools have been sunset.
- **Fail:** The organization subscribes to 5+ analytics tools with overlapping functionality and no rationalization plan.

### 8. Hiring Prioritizes Analytical Thinking Over Tool Expertise
- **Pass:** Job descriptions for analytics roles emphasize critical thinking, statistical literacy, business acumen, and communication over specific tool certifications.
- **Fail:** Job postings require "5 years of [specific tool] experience" as the primary qualification.

### 9. Insights Per Analyst Per Quarter Is Tracked
- **Pass:** There is a lightweight mechanism to count actionable insights delivered per analyst (not as a punitive metric, but as a capacity indicator).
- **Fail:** Analyst output is measured only in report count or dashboard count, not in insight or decision impact.

### 10. Executive Sponsor for Analytics Exists
- **Pass:** A senior leader (VP or above) champions analytics investment, advocates for people budget, and holds the organization accountable for acting on data.
- **Fail:** Analytics is an orphan function with no executive sponsor, making budget requests difficult and insights easy to ignore.
