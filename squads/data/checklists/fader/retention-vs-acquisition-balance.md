# Retention vs. Acquisition Balance

## Purpose

Audit whether marketing and growth investment is appropriately balanced between acquiring new customers and retaining/developing existing ones. Fader argues most companies over-invest in acquisition and under-invest in retention, especially for their best customers. This checklist forces an honest accounting.

---

## Checklist Items

### 1. Acquisition and Retention Budgets Are Separately Tracked
- **Pass:** The organization can state its acquisition spend and retention spend as distinct line items, including headcount, technology, and program costs.
- **Fail:** All spending is lumped under "marketing" with no acquisition/retention breakdown.

### 2. The Ratio Is Known and Benchmarked
- **Pass:** The acquisition-to-retention spend ratio is documented (e.g., 75/25) and compared to industry benchmarks and the company's own customer base composition.
- **Fail:** Nobody knows the ratio, or it has never been compared to any reference point.

### 3. Retention ROI Is Measured
- **Pass:** Retention programs have measurable ROI: incremental revenue retained, churn reduction, and cost of the retention program. ROI is calculated and reviewed quarterly.
- **Fail:** Retention spending is treated as a cost center with no ROI measurement.

### 4. Acquisition CAC Includes Full Cost
- **Pass:** Customer Acquisition Cost includes media spend, sales costs, onboarding costs, and any introductory discounts or promotions. It is computed per channel and per segment.
- **Fail:** CAC includes only media spend, understating the true cost of acquisition.

### 5. CLV-to-CAC Ratio Is Monitored by Segment
- **Pass:** CLV:CAC ratio is calculated per customer segment and per acquisition channel. Ratios below 3:1 trigger review.
- **Fail:** CLV:CAC is a single company-wide average, hiding unprofitable segments or channels.

### 6. High-Value Customer Retention Gets Priority Investment
- **Pass:** Retention investment is disproportionately allocated to the highest-value customer segments, reflecting their outsized revenue contribution.
- **Fail:** Retention efforts (if they exist) are spread equally across all customers regardless of value.

### 7. Acquisition Quality Is Measured, Not Just Volume
- **Pass:** New customer cohorts are tracked for 6-12 month retention and CLV, not just initial conversion. Channels that bring low-quality customers are identified.
- **Fail:** Acquisition is measured only by new customer count or first-purchase revenue, ignoring downstream quality.

### 8. There Is a "Do Not Acquire" or "Deprioritize" List
- **Pass:** Segments or channels known to produce unprofitable customers have been deprioritized or excluded from acquisition targeting.
- **Fail:** The organization acquires every possible customer regardless of expected value.

### 9. Cross-Sell and Upsell Are Part of the Retention Strategy
- **Pass:** Existing customer development (cross-sell, upsell, increased usage) is a defined program with targets, not an afterthought.
- **Fail:** Retention means "prevent churn" only, with no proactive value-growth strategy for existing customers.

### 10. Balance Is Reviewed Annually Against Customer Base Maturity
- **Pass:** As the customer base matures (larger installed base, slower market growth), the balance shifts toward retention. This shift is deliberate and documented.
- **Fail:** The acquisition/retention split has not changed in years despite significant changes in the customer base or market.
