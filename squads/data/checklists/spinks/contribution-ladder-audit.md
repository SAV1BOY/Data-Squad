# Contribution Ladder Audit

## Purpose

Audit the progression path from lurker to contributor to leader within the community. Spinks emphasizes that communities thrive when there is a visible, achievable ladder that rewards increasing contribution. Without it, communities depend on a handful of super-contributors who eventually burn out.

---

## Checklist Items

### 1. Contribution Levels Are Defined
- **Pass:** At least three contribution levels are documented: observer/lurker, active contributor, and leader/ambassador. Each level has a clear description of what the member does at that stage.
- **Fail:** There is no defined progression. Members are either "in" or "not in" with no gradation.

### 2. Each Level Has Clear Entry Criteria
- **Pass:** The requirements to move from one level to the next are transparent and achievable (e.g., "Post 5 helpful answers" to move from lurker to contributor, "Mentor 3 new members" to become a leader).
- **Fail:** Advancement is arbitrary, invisible, or based on relationships with community staff rather than visible contribution.

### 3. The First Step Is Easy
- **Pass:** The first contribution a lurker can make is low-effort and low-risk: introducing themselves, reacting to a post, answering a simple question, or completing a profile. This step is actively encouraged.
- **Fail:** The first meaningful action requires high effort or social risk, creating a barrier that keeps lurkers passive.

### 4. Each Level Has Distinct Benefits
- **Pass:** Higher contribution levels unlock meaningful benefits: recognition (badges, titles), access (private channels, events, early access), influence (voting on topics, advising on community direction), or tangible rewards.
- **Fail:** Contributing more produces no visible benefit, removing the incentive to advance.

### 5. Recognition Is Systematic, Not Just Organic
- **Pass:** There is a system for recognizing contributors: automated badges, community spotlights, thank-you posts, leaderboards, or nomination programs. Recognition happens regularly.
- **Fail:** Recognition depends on community staff noticing contributions, which is inconsistent and misses many contributors.

### 6. The Lurker-to-Contributor Conversion Rate Is Measured
- **Pass:** The team tracks what percentage of registered members make at least one contribution (post, reply, reaction) within their first 30 days. This rate is a key community health metric.
- **Fail:** Lurker-to-contributor conversion is not measured. The community may have 10,000 members with only 50 active ones, and nobody tracks the gap.

### 7. Leader Burnout Is Monitored and Prevented
- **Pass:** Top contributors are monitored for burnout signals (declining activity, shorter posts, negative sentiment). The community team proactively checks in and offers support or respite.
- **Fail:** Super-contributors are taken for granted until they disappear, leaving a vacuum.

### 8. The Ladder Accommodates Different Contribution Types
- **Pass:** The ladder recognizes multiple forms of contribution: content creation, answering questions, welcoming newcomers, event organizing, providing feedback. Not just posting volume.
- **Fail:** Only one type of contribution (e.g., posting) is recognized, marginalizing members who contribute in other valuable ways.

### 9. Leadership Roles Have Responsibilities and Support
- **Pass:** Community leaders/ambassadors have documented responsibilities, training/onboarding, regular check-ins with staff, and clear expectations. It is a role, not just a title.
- **Fail:** Leaders receive a badge but no guidance, training, or support. They are expected to figure it out.

### 10. The Ladder Is Visible to All Members
- **Pass:** The progression path is documented and accessible to all members. A new member can see what levels exist, what it takes to advance, and what benefits each level offers.
- **Fail:** The ladder exists in the community team's internal documents but is not visible to members.
