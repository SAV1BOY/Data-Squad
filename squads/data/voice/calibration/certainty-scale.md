# Certainty Scale

## Purpose

This scale provides standardized language for expressing how confident we are in a finding. Using consistent certainty language prevents miscommunication where one person's "we think" is another person's "we know." Every data communication should signal its confidence level.

## The Scale

### Level 1: Hypothesis — "We suspect"

**Confidence**: Less than 50%. An informed guess based on pattern recognition, anecdote, or preliminary signals.
**Evidence basis**: No formal analysis. Based on intuition, analogies, or early directional data.
**Appropriate action**: Investigate further. Do not make decisions based on this alone.

**Standard phrases**:
- "Our working hypothesis is..."
- "Early signals suggest, but we have not validated..."
- "Based on anecdotal evidence, we suspect..."
- "This is a preliminary observation that requires further analysis."
- "We have a directional sense that... but no quantitative confirmation yet."

**Usage note**: Always pair with a plan to validate. A hypothesis without a path to evidence is just speculation.

### Level 2: Directional — "We believe"

**Confidence**: 50-75%. The data leans in a direction, but the evidence is not yet conclusive.
**Evidence basis**: Initial analysis, small sample, or early experiment data. Multiple signals pointing the same way.
**Appropriate action**: Proceed with caution. Plan around this direction but have a contingency.

**Standard phrases**:
- "The data directionally supports..."
- "We believe, based on initial analysis, that..."
- "Current evidence leans toward... though the sample is limited."
- "Preliminary results indicate... We will confirm with a larger dataset by [date]."
- "Multiple signals point in this direction, but we have not ruled out alternative explanations."

**Usage note**: State what would change your mind. Name the threshold or evidence that would move this to confirmed or rejected.

### Level 3: Probable — "The data shows"

**Confidence**: 75-95%. The analysis is complete and the evidence is strong, but not yet at the highest bar of proof.
**Evidence basis**: Complete analysis with adequate sample, statistical significance on primary metric, or consistent multi-source evidence.
**Appropriate action**: Act on this with reasonable confidence. Monitor for disconfirming signals.

**Standard phrases**:
- "The data shows..."
- "Our analysis confirms with [X]% confidence that..."
- "Based on a sample of [n] over [time period], the evidence supports..."
- "The trend is consistent across segments and time periods."
- "We are confident that... with the caveat that [specific limitation]."

**Usage note**: Include the specific confidence level, sample size, and time period. "The data shows" without supporting detail is a claim, not evidence.

### Level 4: Confirmed — "We know"

**Confidence**: Greater than 95%. The finding has been validated through rigorous methodology, replicated, or established through multiple independent analyses.
**Evidence basis**: Statistically significant results (p<0.05), replicated across cohorts or time periods, validated by multiple analysts or methods.
**Appropriate action**: Act with high confidence. Use this as a basis for strategic decisions.

**Standard phrases**:
- "We know with high confidence that..."
- "This has been validated through [method] and is statistically significant at p<0.05."
- "This finding has been replicated across [X] cohorts and [Y] time periods."
- "This is an established fact in our data: [finding]."
- "We can state definitively that... based on [specific evidence]."

**Usage note**: Reserve "we know" for genuinely validated findings. Overusing it erodes trust in all your communications.

### Level 5: Established Fact — "This is how it works"

**Confidence**: Effectively 100%. Foundational knowledge validated repeatedly over extended time periods.
**Evidence basis**: Years of consistent data, industry-standard metrics, or definitional truths.
**Appropriate action**: Treat as a given. Challenge only with extraordinary evidence.

**Standard phrases**:
- "Our data consistently shows, across all measured periods, that..."
- "This is a well-established pattern in our business."
- "By definition, [metric] measures [what it measures]."

**Usage note**: Very few findings reach this level. Most things we "know" are actually Level 3 or 4.

## Quick Reference

| Level | Label | Confidence | Key Phrase | Action Implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hypothesis | <50% | "We suspect" | Investigate |
| 2 | Directional | 50-75% | "We believe" | Proceed cautiously |
| 3 | Probable | 75-95% | "The data shows" | Act with confidence |
| 4 | Confirmed | >95% | "We know" | Act decisively |
| 5 | Established | ~100% | "This is how it works" | Treat as given |
