# First Round Review: Growth and Data Articles

Index of the most valuable articles from First Round Review on growth, data, and analytics.

---

## Growth Strategy

- **"The Only Metric That Matters"** (Josh Elman) - Argument for focusing on a single core engagement metric rather than a dashboard of KPIs. Uses LinkedIn, Twitter, and Medium as case studies. Introduces the concept of "core action" -- the single behavior that defines an engaged user.
- **"Growth is Good, Smart Growth is Better"** - Framework for evaluating growth quality: are new users retaining? Is CAC sustainable? Is the growth loop compounding? Case study from a consumer app that grew to 10M users but retained only 4%.
- **"How Superhuman Built an Engine to Find Product-Market Fit"** (Rahul Vohra) - Detailed walkthrough of using the "very disappointed" survey methodology to quantitatively measure and improve product-market fit. Includes the scoring system, segmentation approach, and iterative improvement process.

## Data and Analytics

- **"The Dangerous Seduction of the Lifetime Value Formula"** (Bill Gurley) - Classic article warning against over-reliance on LTV calculations. Covers how LTV models break down: assumptions about retention rates, discount rates, and margin sustainability. Essential reading for anyone building unit economics models.
- **"What I Learned from Analyzing 3,000 Growth Experiments"** - Meta-analysis of experiment results across Reforge portfolio companies. Key findings: 70% of experiments fail, winning experiments average 2-5% lift, and the best experiment programs optimize for learning velocity, not win rate.
- **"The Power of Product Thinking"** (Julie Zhuo) - How to frame analytics around product decisions rather than metric dashboards. The difference between "data-informed" and "data-driven" organizations.
- **"How to Negotiate with Metrics"** - Guide for data leaders on using metrics in strategic conversations: choosing the right metric for the audience, framing data as options (not answers), and handling metric disagreements.

## Retention and Engagement

- **"Why Retention is the Silent Killer of Startups"** - Quantitative analysis showing that most startups focus on acquisition while retention determines long-term success. Includes a retention health check framework with benchmarks by vertical.
- **"Your Engagement Metrics are Lying to You"** - How aggregate engagement metrics (DAU, sessions) can mask underlying problems. Introduces the "engagement distribution" concept: analyzing the full distribution of user activity, not just averages.
- **"Building Products That Stick"** - How to identify and reinforce the habit loops in your product. Case studies from Instacart (weekly grocery habit), Duolingo (daily learning streak), and Slack (work communication default).

## Pricing and Monetization

- **"Everything We've Learned About Pricing"** - Compilation of pricing lessons from First Round portfolio companies. Covers value-based pricing, pricing page design, enterprise pricing negotiation, and when to raise prices.
- **"The Science of Freemium"** - When freemium works and when it doesn't. Framework for designing free tiers that drive conversion without cannibalizing paid plans. Case studies from Dropbox, Evernote, and Zoom.

## Team and Process

- **"How Stripe Measures Everything"** - Inside Stripe's approach to company metrics: weekly business reviews, metric ownership, and the "metrics tree" that connects individual team goals to company-level KPIs.
- **"Building Your First Data Team"** - Practical guide for founders hiring their first data person: what skills to look for, where the role should sit, and the first 90-day plan. Covers the "analytics engineer" role before it was widely known.
- **"The Art of the Postmortem"** - How to conduct effective experiment and incident postmortems that generate learning. Template for documenting what happened, why, and what changes are needed.

## Customer Success and Sales

- **"The Customer Success Metric That Matters Most"** - Argument for NRR as the single most important CS metric, with benchmarks by company stage and strategies for improving each component (retention, expansion, contraction).
