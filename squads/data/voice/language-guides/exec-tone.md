# Executive Tone Language Guide

## Purpose

This guide defines how the data team communicates with executives. The core principle: every communication should fit on one slide of truth. Executives need the answer, the implication, and the recommended action. They do not need the journey.

## Core Principles

1. **Lead with the headline**: State the most important finding in the first sentence
2. **One slide, one truth**: If you cannot fit it on one slide, you have not distilled it enough
3. **Metrics tied to goals**: Always reference the company OKR or strategic priority the data connects to
4. **Recommend, do not just report**: Executives expect a point of view, not a data dump
5. **Quantify impact**: Translate every finding into dollars, users, or time
6. **Anticipate the follow-up**: Address the obvious next question before it is asked

## Structure for Executive Communications

### Written (Email / Document)
- **Line 1**: The headline finding or recommendation
- **Line 2-3**: The key supporting data points (2-3 maximum)
- **Line 4**: The business implication
- **Line 5**: The recommended action and timeline
- **Appendix** (optional): Methodology, caveats, raw data for those who want depth

### Verbal (Meeting / Presentation)
- Open with: "The one thing to know is..."
- Support with: "The data behind this is..."
- Close with: "Our recommendation is... because..."
- Reserve detail for Q&A, do not front-load it

## Language Patterns

| Instead of | Use |
|---|---|
| "We ran an analysis and found several interesting trends" | "Retention is down 8%. Here is why and what to do." |
| "The data suggests a possible correlation" | "Users who complete onboarding retain at 2x the rate" |
| "There are multiple factors at play" | "The primary driver is X. Secondary factors are Y and Z." |
| "We need more data to be sure" | "Current data points to X with 85% confidence. We will confirm by Friday." |
| "The experiment had mixed results" | "The experiment improved activation by 12% but reduced conversion by 3%. Net impact is positive." |

## Formatting Rules

- No walls of text. Use bullets, bold key numbers, and whitespace.
- Bold the single most important number in any communication.
- Tables over paragraphs when comparing options or scenarios.
- If including a chart, annotate the one thing the viewer should take away.
- Keep email subject lines actionable: "Action needed: Q2 retention risk" not "Data Update."

## Common Mistakes

- Sending a 10-slide deck when a 3-line Slack message would suffice
- Including technical methodology in the main body instead of an appendix
- Presenting data without a recommendation
- Using analyst jargon: "p-value," "cohort decomposition," "regression coefficient"
- Burying the bad news or softening it so much the urgency is lost
- Providing too many options without a clear recommendation
