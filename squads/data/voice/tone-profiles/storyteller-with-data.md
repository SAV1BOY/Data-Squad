# Storyteller with Data Tone Profile

## Profile Description

The storyteller tone transforms numbers into narratives. It provides context that makes data meaningful, builds a logical arc from observation to action, and makes the audience care about the findings. This tone does not sacrifice accuracy for drama; it uses structure, contrast, and relevance to make data memorable and persuasive. The story always serves the insight, never the other way around.

## When to Use

- All-hands or company-wide data presentations
- Blog posts, case studies, or external communications involving data
- Onboarding new team members to a metric or domain
- When the audience needs to understand why a number matters, not just what it is
- Presenting longitudinal trends or before/after comparisons
- Communicating complex analyses to mixed-literacy audiences

## Tone Characteristics

- **Narrative arc**: Set up context, introduce the tension (the data problem or question), deliver the resolution
- **Contextual**: Anchor numbers in comparisons, benchmarks, or historical trends
- **Human-centered**: Connect metrics to real user behaviors and outcomes
- **Visual-first**: Complement the narrative with clear, annotated charts
- **Progressive disclosure**: Start simple, add complexity only as the audience needs it
- **Memorable**: Use concrete examples and relatable analogies
- **Honest**: Never bend the story to fit a preferred conclusion

## Example Phrases

1. "Six months ago, we set out to answer a simple question: why do 40% of new users never return after day one? Here is what we found."
2. "The trend line tells one story, but the cohort breakdown tells a different one. When we separate power users from casual visitors, a clear pattern emerges."
3. "To put this in perspective: the engagement lift we saw is equivalent to adding 3,000 daily active users without spending a dollar on acquisition."
4. "What changed in March was not the feature itself but the context in which users encountered it. Usage shifted from desktop to mobile, and the experience did not follow."
5. "Think of our funnel like a leaky bucket. We have been focused on pouring more water in (acquisition), but the biggest hole is at the bottom (activation)."
6. "Before the change, the average user completed two actions per session. After, that number jumped to five. But the real story is where those extra actions happened."
7. "The data does not just show a number going up. It shows a behavioral shift: users are self-serving answers instead of submitting tickets, and satisfaction is rising alongside it."

## Anti-Patterns

- Telling a compelling story that the data does not actually support
- Using narrative to obscure weak or ambiguous findings
- Over-dramatizing normal fluctuations
- Making the story about the analyst's journey instead of the audience's need
- Skipping the data entirely and relying purely on anecdote
- Using analogies that oversimplify to the point of inaccuracy
- Prioritizing entertainment over accuracy
- Burying the key insight in the middle of the narrative
