# Workshop Kit: Community Analytics Fundamentals

## Workshop Overview

**Duration:** 3 hours
**Audience:** Community managers, developer relations, customer success managers, marketing team members, and analysts supporting community programs
**Group size:** 8-20 participants
**Prerequisite:** Basic familiarity with community platforms (forum, Slack, Discord, or similar)
**Goal:** Participants leave able to define community metrics, build measurement frameworks, demonstrate community ROI, and avoid common measurement pitfalls

## Facilitator Preparation

### Materials Needed
- Whiteboard or digital canvas
- Access to your community platform's analytics (or exported data)
- Printed copies of the SPACES framework reference sheet
- Exercise worksheets (one per participant)
- Sample community data set for exercises (can be anonymized real data or generated)

### Pre-Workshop Preparation
- Export 3-6 months of community activity data (posts, replies, active members, new members)
- Prepare 3 visualizations from the data: member growth trend, activity trend, and response rate trend
- Identify 2-3 community "success stories" (members who became advocates, questions that deflected support tickets, feedback that influenced the product)
- Calculate current community cost (platform fees, staff time, program costs) for ROI exercise

## Session Plan

### Module 1: Why Community Analytics Matter (30 minutes)

**Opening discussion (10 min):**
Ask participants: "If someone asked you to prove the community is worth its investment, what would you show them?" Collect answers on the board. Most will be activity metrics (posts, members, page views). Note this -- the workshop will move from activity metrics to value metrics.

**The community measurement problem (10 min):**

Communities suffer from a unique measurement challenge: their value is diffuse. A support ticket deflected, a customer who did not churn because of community connections, a product insight from a community discussion -- these are real but hard to attribute. The result is that communities are either:
- Measured by vanity metrics (member count) that do not demonstrate value
- Not measured at all, making them vulnerable to budget cuts
- Over-claimed (all retention improvement attributed to community) which damages credibility

The solution is a structured measurement framework that connects community activity to business outcomes with honest attribution.

**The SPACES overview (10 min):**
Introduce the SPACES framework (Support, Product, Acquisition, Contribution, Engagement, Success). Explain that most communities deliver value in 1-2 primary SPACES categories, and measurement should focus on these primary value drivers.

### Module 2: Defining Your Community Metrics (60 minutes)

**Exercise 1: Identify your community's primary value (15 min):**

Have each participant (or team, if multiple people manage the same community) answer:
1. Why does our community exist? (In one sentence)
2. What is the #1 way it creates business value? (Support deflection? Acquisition? Retention? Product feedback?)
3. What is the #2 way?

Share answers. Facilitate alignment if there is disagreement -- a community with no clear primary value proposition has a strategy problem, not just a measurement problem.

**Building a metric framework (25 min):**

Using the primary and secondary SPACES values identified above, guide participants through selecting metrics at three levels:

**Level 1: Health metrics (Is the community alive and growing?)**
- Monthly Active Members (MAM)
- New member join rate
- Member retention (30-day, 90-day)
- Response rate (% of posts that receive a reply)
- Time to first response

**Level 2: Activity metrics (Is the community generating the right activity?)**
- Posts per active member
- Questions asked and answered
- Content created by members vs. staff
- Event attendance and participation
- Contributor diversity (how many unique contributors)

**Level 3: Value metrics (Is the community generating business value?)**
- Ticket deflection count and dollar value (Support)
- Feature requests sourced from community (Product)
- New customers attributed to community (Acquisition)
- Retention rate of community members vs. non-members (Success)
- User-generated content page views and search traffic (Contribution)

**Exercise (20 min):** Have each participant select 2 metrics from each level for their community. For each metric, define:
- Exact definition (what counts, what does not)
- Data source (where will you get this number)
- Current baseline (if known)
- Target for next quarter

### Module 3: Measuring Community ROI (45 minutes)

**The ROI framework (15 min):**

Community ROI = Total Community Value / Total Community Cost

**Cost side (relatively straightforward):**
- Community platform fees
- Staff time (community managers, moderators) as a percentage of salary
- Program costs (events, swag, tools)
- Content creation costs

**Value side (requires attribution):**
Walk through how to quantify each SPACES dimension:

**Support value:**
Count community questions answered without a support ticket. Multiply by average ticket cost.
Formula: Community-answered questions x Average support ticket cost
Example: 200 questions/month x $15/ticket = $3,000/month in deflected support costs

**Product value:**
Track feature requests that originated in the community and were implemented. Value is harder to quantify -- use engineering time saved on user research as a proxy.
Formula: Community-sourced features x Average user research cost per feature
Example: 4 features/quarter x $5,000 research cost = $20,000/quarter

**Acquisition value:**
Track new customers who engaged with community before purchasing. Apply your standard CPA to these customers as the value community provided.
Formula: Community-attributed new customers x Average CPA
Example: 30 customers/quarter x $200 CPA = $6,000/quarter

**Success/Retention value:**
Compare retention rates of community-engaged customers vs. non-engaged. Apply the retention delta to community-engaged customer revenue.
Formula: (Retention delta) x (Number of community members) x (Average revenue per customer)
Example: 5% retention improvement x 500 community customers x $1,200 annual revenue = $30,000/year

**Exercise (20 min):** Have participants calculate a rough ROI for their community using the framework above. Even rough estimates are valuable -- they identify which value dimensions are largest and where better measurement is needed.

**Honest attribution discussion (10 min):**
Facilitate a discussion on the limits of community attribution:
- Selection bias: community members may be inherently more engaged
- Attribution overlap: a customer may have been influenced by community AND marketing
- Time lag: community value often accrues over months, not weeks

Introduce the concept of "conservative estimates" -- when in doubt, attribute less value to community. Credibility is more important than impressive numbers.

### Module 4: Building Your Measurement Practice (45 minutes)

**The community analytics maturity model (15 min):**

**Stage 1: Basic counting (Month 1-2)**
Track member count, post count, active members. Use platform-native analytics. No integration with business systems. Report monthly.

**Stage 2: Engagement measurement (Month 3-4)**
Track response rates, time to first response, contributor diversity. Calculate member retention. Start segmenting by member type (new vs. tenured, customer vs. non-customer).

**Stage 3: Value attribution (Month 5-8)**
Connect community data to business systems (CRM, support platform). Calculate ticket deflection, track community-sourced product feedback. Begin comparing community member vs. non-member outcomes.

**Stage 4: ROI and optimization (Month 9+)**
Calculate full ROI. Run experiments on community programs (events, content series, engagement tactics). Optimize investment based on which activities drive the most value.

**Building a community dashboard (15 min):**

Design a community dashboard together using the principles from the Dashboard Design workshop:

**Executive view (1 page):**
- Community ROI (single number with trend)
- Top 3 value metrics (ticket deflection, acquisition, retention)
- Member health summary (active members, growth rate, response rate)

**Operational view (for community managers):**
- Daily/weekly activity metrics
- Member engagement segments (champions, at-risk, new, lurking)
- Content performance (most viewed, most engaged, unanswered questions)
- Upcoming actions (members to welcome, questions to answer, events to promote)

**Exercise (15 min):** Sketch a community dashboard for your community. Include no more than 7 metrics. For each metric, note where the data comes from and who will act on it.

## Post-Workshop Resources

### Community Metrics Quick Reference

**Report monthly to leadership:**
- Monthly Active Members and growth rate
- Community ROI (total value / total cost)
- Top 2-3 value metrics for your primary SPACES dimensions

**Track weekly for operations:**
- New members joined
- Response rate and time to first response
- Top contributors and their activity
- Unanswered questions queue

**Review quarterly for strategy:**
- Member retention cohorts
- Value attribution accuracy (are your estimates holding up?)
- Program ROI (which community activities drive the most value?)
- Comparison of community member vs. non-member outcomes

### Common Pitfalls to Avoid

| Pitfall | Why It Is Tempting | Why It Is Wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Reporting total member count | Big number, always goes up | Most members are inactive; active members matter |
| Claiming all retention lift | Makes community look very valuable | Selection bias means you are overclaiming |
| Measuring only activity | Activity is easy to count | Activity without outcomes is noise |
| Comparing to social media benchmarks | Other teams use social metrics | Community operates differently; use community benchmarks |
| Skipping the cost side of ROI | Makes ROI look better | Incomplete ROI damages credibility when challenged |

### Recommended Next Steps
1. Define your community's primary SPACES value within 1 week
2. Select 5-7 metrics across the three levels (health, activity, value) within 2 weeks
3. Build a basic community dashboard within 1 month
4. Calculate your first community ROI estimate within 2 months
5. Present community ROI to leadership within 3 months

## Measuring Workshop Impact

**Immediate:** Post-workshop survey on confidence in community measurement (1-5 scale)
**30-day:** Number of participants who have defined community metrics using the framework
**90-day:** Number of communities with active ROI tracking
**6-month:** Change in leadership perception of community value (survey)
