# Web Analytics 2.0 - Avinash Kaushik (2009)

## Book Info

- **Title:** Web Analytics 2.0: The Art of Online Accountability and Science of Customer Centricity
- **Author:** Avinash Kaushik
- **Publisher:** Sybex / Wiley
- **Year:** 2009
- **ISBN:** 978-0470529393
- **Pages:** 475
- **Context:** Follow-up to Kaushik's first book, written when Google Analytics was maturing and the industry was shifting from hit counters to strategic measurement. Kaushik was Google's Digital Marketing Evangelist.

## Core Thesis

Traditional web analytics (clickstream data alone) captures only a fraction of digital reality. True insight demands a multiplicity framework combining four complementary data sources: clickstream data (the "what"), outcomes analysis (the "how much"), experimentation and testing (the "why"), and voice of customer / qualitative data (the "why behind the why"). Organizations that rely solely on clickstream are doomed to report activity without understanding intent, cause, or value.

## Key Concepts

### 1. The Web Analytics 2.0 Multiplicity Model
The four pillars: (a) Clickstream data -- visits, pageviews, paths, funnels. (b) Multiple outcomes analysis -- macro and micro conversions tied to economic value. (c) Experimentation and testing -- A/B and multivariate tests to isolate causation. (d) Voice of customer -- surveys, usability studies, and qualitative feedback to understand motivation and satisfaction. No single pillar is sufficient; competitive advantage comes from layering all four.

### 2. The 10/90 Rule
Kaushik's most famous heuristic: for every $100 you spend on analytics, invest $10 on tools and $90 on the people who interpret the data. The bottleneck is never the technology; it is the analytical talent, curiosity, and organizational willingness to act on insights.

### 3. Macro and Micro Conversions
Macro conversions are the primary business goals (purchase, lead, signup). Micro conversions are smaller intent signals (newsletter subscribe, video view, PDF download) that indicate engagement and potential future value. Tracking only macros misses the majority of visitor intent; tracking only micros drowns in vanity metrics. Both must be measured and valued.

### 4. Economic Value
Every micro and macro conversion should have an assigned economic value so that traffic sources, content, and campaigns can be compared on a common currency. Without economic value, analytics devolves into counting things rather than measuring business impact.

### 5. The Trinity Strategy
An earlier framework Kaushik refined into the multiplicity model: Behavior Analysis (what happened), Outcomes Analysis (what resulted), and Experience Analysis (why it happened). The trinity ensures analysts never get stuck in one mode of investigation.

### 6. Critical Few vs. Actionable Metrics
Kaushik rails against "data puking" -- the practice of dumping 50-page reports full of metrics nobody acts on. Instead, identify the critical few KPIs that directly connect to business outcomes, and ensure every report answers "so what?" and recommends a specific action.

### 7. Competitive Intelligence
Use tools like Hitwise, Compete, and search analytics to understand relative performance. Internal data only tells you about your visitors; competitive intelligence tells you about the market you are missing.

### 8. Segmentation or Death
Aggregate data is useless. The only path to insight is segmentation: by traffic source, visitor type (new vs returning), geography, behavior cluster, campaign, landing page, and outcome. "Segment or die" is Kaushik's mantra.

## Application to Data Squad

- **Multiplicity as audit framework:** When evaluating a company's analytics maturity, check which of the four pillars are present. Most early-stage companies have clickstream only; the gap analysis reveals the roadmap.
- **10/90 rule in staffing:** Data Squad should advocate that clients invest in analyst capability, not just tool licenses. A sophisticated tool with no analyst is worse than a spreadsheet with a curious human.
- **Economic value discipline:** Every dashboard Data Squad builds should force the conversation about assigning dollar values to micro conversions. This is where analytics connects to CLV (see Fader).
- **Segmentation as default:** No Data Squad deliverable should present aggregate-only data. Every metric should be accompanied by at least one meaningful segment view.
- **Experimentation maturity:** Use the multiplicity model to assess where a client sits on the experimentation spectrum and build a testing roadmap alongside the measurement one.
- **Voice of customer integration:** Encourage clients to pair quantitative dashboards with qualitative research (surveys, session recordings, interviews) so that data tells the "what" and qualitative tells the "why."
