# Data Thought Leadership Strategy: Topics, Channels, and Cadence

## Purpose

This guide provides a complete strategy for establishing thought leadership in the data and analytics space. It covers what to talk about, where to publish, how often, and how to measure impact. Thought leadership is not vanity publishing -- it is a strategic investment that attracts talent, builds credibility with stakeholders, and creates opportunities for partnerships and business development.

## Topic Strategy

### Tier 1: Core Expertise Topics (60% of content)
These are topics where your team has deep, hands-on experience and can share original insights.

**Recommended topics:**
- Metric design and measurement frameworks for specific business contexts
- Experimentation methodology: practical lessons from running real experiments
- Data quality: systems, processes, and war stories
- Dashboard design: principles that connect data to decisions
- Analytics engineering: dbt patterns, data modeling, pipeline architecture
- Customer analytics: CLV modeling, cohort analysis, retention analysis

**Content format:** Deep, technical articles (1,500-3,000 words), case studies, code walkthroughs, methodology comparisons.

### Tier 2: Industry Perspective Topics (25% of content)
These are topics where you offer informed opinions on trends, tools, and methodologies.

**Recommended topics:**
- The evolving role of the data analyst in product organizations
- Privacy-first analytics: practical implications and strategies
- AI/ML in analytics: where it helps, where it overpromises
- The modern data stack: honest assessments of tools and architecture
- Data literacy: building data-driven culture in non-technical teams
- Metrics that matter vs. vanity metrics across industries

**Content format:** Opinion pieces (800-1,500 words), trend analyses, framework comparisons, contrarian takes with evidence.

### Tier 3: Community and Curation Topics (15% of content)
Sharing and amplifying others' work, industry roundups, and community building.

**Recommended topics:**
- Weekly or monthly roundups of the best data content
- Book reviews and summaries of data-related publications
- Tool reviews and comparisons based on hands-on experience
- Interview-style content with practitioners from other organizations
- Event recaps and conference takeaways

**Content format:** Short posts (300-800 words), social media threads, newsletter issues.

## Channel Strategy

### Primary Channels (invest heavily)

**1. Long-form blog or publication**
- Platform: Company blog, Substack, or Medium publication
- Cadence: 2 articles per month
- Purpose: Depth, SEO, reference material
- Metric: Monthly unique readers, time on page, email subscribers

**2. LinkedIn**
- Cadence: 3-4 posts per week
- Mix: 2 original posts, 1 article share with commentary, 1 engagement post (question, poll, or response to others)
- Purpose: Professional network reach, credibility with stakeholders and potential hires
- Metric: Impressions, engagement rate, profile views, connection requests

**3. Email newsletter**
- Cadence: Biweekly or monthly
- Content: Curated insights, original analysis, upcoming events
- Purpose: Owned audience, direct relationship
- Metric: Subscriber count, open rate, click rate

### Secondary Channels (moderate investment)

**4. Twitter/X**
- Cadence: Daily engagement, 2-3 original threads per month
- Purpose: Data community engagement, real-time discussions
- Metric: Followers, thread engagement, mentions

**5. Conference speaking**
- Cadence: 2-4 talks per year (see conference-speaking-strategy.md)
- Purpose: Credibility, networking, content generation
- Metric: Invitations received, audience feedback, leads generated

**6. Podcast appearances**
- Cadence: 1-2 per quarter
- Purpose: Reach new audiences, deeper topic exploration
- Metric: Listener feedback, referral traffic

### Experimental Channels (light investment)

**7. YouTube or video content**
- Cadence: 1 per month (can repurpose conference talks)
- Purpose: Tutorial content, methodology demonstrations
- Metric: Views, subscribers, watch time

**8. Community participation**
- Platforms: dbt Community, Locally Optimistic, Data Twitter, Reddit r/analytics
- Cadence: Weekly participation
- Purpose: Credibility within practitioner community
- Metric: Reputation, mentions, inbound requests

## Measuring Thought Leadership Impact

### Vanity Metrics (track but do not optimize for)
- Follower counts, page views, social media impressions

### Leading Indicators (track and optimize)
- Email subscriber growth rate
- Inbound speaking invitations
- Inbound partnership or collaboration requests
- Content engagement rate (comments, shares, saves vs. views)
- Mentions by other thought leaders

### Business Impact Metrics (the ones that matter)
- Inbound talent applications citing content as a factor
- Stakeholder references to your content in internal conversations
- Client or partner conversations initiated through content
- Media or analyst mentions

## Content Production Process

1. **Ideation (ongoing)**: Maintain a content idea backlog. Add ideas from daily work, conversations, conference talks, and industry reading.
2. **Selection (monthly)**: Choose next month's topics based on timeliness, expertise depth, and audience interest.
3. **Drafting (weekly)**: Write first drafts during dedicated Thursday writing time.
4. **Review (before publish)**: Have a colleague review for accuracy, clarity, and tone.
5. **Publishing (per schedule)**: Publish on the planned date with cross-promotion across channels.
6. **Amplification (post-publish)**: Share across channels over 1-2 weeks with different angles and excerpts.
7. **Measurement (monthly)**: Review metrics and adjust strategy based on what resonates.

## Key Principles

1. **Consistency beats virality.** Publishing regularly for 12 months builds more credibility than one viral post.
2. **Share real work, not abstract advice.** The most compelling thought leadership comes from sharing what you actually did, including failures.
3. **Be generous with knowledge.** Give away your best frameworks and methods. The value is in the execution, not the secret.
4. **Take positions.** Thought leadership requires opinions. Bland, consensus content does not build a following.
5. **Engage with others' work.** Thought leadership is a conversation, not a broadcast. Comment on, build upon, and respectfully disagree with others.
6. **Measure what matters.** Follower counts are vanity. Inbound opportunities are impact.
