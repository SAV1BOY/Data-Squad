# Spinks Community Business Case Audit

## Purpose

Audit the business case for the community program: does it demonstrate ROI with defensible evidence, or is it a faith-based initiative? Spinks argues that community teams must prove their value in business language, or they will be the first budget cut in a downturn. This checklist ensures the business case is built on solid ground.

---

## Checklist Items

### 1. The Business Case Exists as a Document
- **Pass:** There is a written business case for the community program that states the investment (headcount, tools, programs), the expected returns (retention lift, support deflection, pipeline influence), and the measurement plan.
- **Fail:** The community exists because someone thought it was a good idea, with no formal business case or investment justification.

### 2. Investment Is Fully Accounted For
- **Pass:** The business case includes all costs: community team salaries, platform/tool costs, event costs, content production, moderation time, and internal stakeholder time.
- **Fail:** Only platform costs are counted, significantly understating the true investment.

### 3. Revenue Impact Is Quantified
- **Pass:** Revenue impact is estimated through measurable channels: retention lift (community members retain at X% vs. Y% for non-members), expansion influence, and pipeline contribution from community-sourced leads.
- **Fail:** Revenue impact is claimed in general terms ("community drives loyalty") without specific numbers.

### 4. Cost Savings Are Documented
- **Pass:** Cost savings from support deflection (community-answered questions that would otherwise be tickets), reduced onboarding costs, and crowdsourced content are estimated with methodology.
- **Fail:** Cost savings are assumed but never calculated.

### 5. The Business Case Uses Conservative Assumptions
- **Pass:** Assumptions are explicitly conservative: attribution is partial (community gets credit for a portion, not all), self-selection bias is acknowledged, and estimates use lower-bound scenarios.
- **Fail:** The business case cherry-picks the most favorable interpretation of data to inflate ROI.

### 6. Leading Indicators Show Trajectory
- **Pass:** Even if long-term ROI is hard to prove, leading indicators (engagement growth, member retention, contribution quality, sentiment) show positive trajectory and are linked to business outcomes.
- **Fail:** The business case relies entirely on lagging indicators that will not be available for 12+ months.

### 7. Comparable Investments Are Referenced
- **Pass:** The community ROI is compared to the ROI of alternative investments (paid media, additional CSMs, content marketing) to demonstrate relative value.
- **Fail:** The community business case is presented in isolation with no comparison to alternative uses of the same budget.

### 8. Stakeholder Feedback Validates the Case
- **Pass:** Sales, CS, product, and support leaders have provided input on the community's perceived value. Their endorsement (or constructive criticism) is incorporated.
- **Fail:** The business case was built by the community team alone without cross-functional validation.

### 9. Risks and Failure Conditions Are Stated
- **Pass:** The business case includes what could go wrong: community fails to reach critical mass, engagement drops, moderation costs escalate, or community generates negative sentiment.
- **Fail:** The business case presents only the upside with no discussion of risks or failure scenarios.

### 10. The Business Case Is Updated Annually
- **Pass:** The business case is refreshed at least annually with actual performance data replacing projections. Year-over-year improvement (or decline) is visible.
- **Fail:** The original business case is still in use years later with projections that were never replaced by actuals.

### 11. Executive Sponsorship Is Secured
- **Pass:** A senior leader (VP or above) sponsors the community program, advocates for its budget, and holds the team accountable for delivering on the business case.
- **Fail:** The community team has no executive sponsor, making budget renewals a recurring battle.
