# Engagement Signal Quality

## Purpose

Audit whether community engagement is measured by signals that indicate real depth and health, not vanity metrics. Spinks argues that likes, page views, and member count are shallow proxies. Real engagement signals include replies, help given, returning members, and conversation depth.

---

## Checklist Items

### 1. Depth Metrics Are Tracked Over Volume Metrics
- **Pass:** The primary engagement metrics are depth-oriented: replies per post, conversation thread length, questions answered, members helping members. These take precedence over total posts or page views.
- **Fail:** The community's lead metric is total posts, total members, or page views -- volume metrics that say nothing about quality.

### 2. Member Retention Is a Primary Metric
- **Pass:** The team tracks what percentage of members who were active last month are active again this month (community DAU/MAU equivalent). Cohort-based retention curves are produced.
- **Fail:** Only total member count is tracked, hiding the fact that new signups replace churning members.

### 3. Helper Ratio Is Measured
- **Pass:** The ratio of members who answer questions to members who only ask is tracked. A healthy community has a growing population of helpers, not just askers.
- **Fail:** The team does not distinguish between question-askers and answer-givers.

### 4. Response Time and Response Rate Are Tracked
- **Pass:** The average time to first response on a community post is measured. The percentage of posts that receive at least one reply is tracked. Both are targeted for improvement.
- **Fail:** Nobody measures whether posts get responses. Unanswered posts pile up unnoticed.

### 5. Content Quality Is Assessed, Not Just Quantity
- **Pass:** There is a mechanism (manual review, member upvoting, or automated classification) to distinguish high-quality contributions from low-quality ones. Quality trends are monitored.
- **Fail:** All posts are counted equally regardless of whether they are thoughtful discussions or low-effort noise.

### 6. Returning Member Rate Is a Core Metric
- **Pass:** The percentage of members who return to the community after their first visit is tracked at 7-day, 30-day, and 90-day intervals. This is treated as a retention signal.
- **Fail:** First-visit-to-return conversion is not measured. The community may have high traffic but low return rates.

### 7. Lurker Activity Is Visible
- **Pass:** Even non-posting members are tracked for engagement signals: page views, time on page, content consumption patterns. This reveals the "silent majority" and their engagement depth.
- **Fail:** Only posting behavior is tracked, ignoring the 90% of members who consume but do not post.

### 8. Sentiment Signals Are Monitored
- **Pass:** Community sentiment is tracked through qualitative review, automated sentiment analysis, or periodic surveys. Shifts in tone (positive, negative, frustrated) are flagged.
- **Fail:** Nobody monitors sentiment. A community can turn toxic gradually without the team noticing.

### 9. Engagement Signals Are Segmented
- **Pass:** Engagement metrics are broken down by member type (new vs. tenured), topic area, and platform/channel to identify where engagement is strong and where it is weak.
- **Fail:** Only aggregate engagement numbers are reported, hiding that one topic or segment may be thriving while another is dying.

### 10. Vanity Metrics Are Explicitly Deprioritized
- **Pass:** The team has a documented list of metrics that are tracked for context only (total members, total posts) vs. metrics that drive decisions (retention, helper ratio, response rate).
- **Fail:** Vanity metrics lead the community report, giving a false sense of health.
