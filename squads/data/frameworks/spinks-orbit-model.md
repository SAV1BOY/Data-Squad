# Spinks Orbit Model

## Overview

The Orbit Model is a framework for measuring community health and member engagement based on the
metaphor of gravitational pull. Instead of the traditional funnel (which implies a linear path to
a single endpoint), the Orbit Model envisions community members as bodies in orbit around a central
value -- with different members at different distances, exerting different levels of gravitational
influence. Members closer to the center are more engaged and more influential. The model measures
four dimensions: Gravity (ability to attract new members), Reach (breadth of community awareness),
Impact (depth of individual member contribution), and Love (intensity of member attachment).

## Origin

Developed by **David Spinks** and the team at Orbit (the company), building on Spinks's community
management philosophy from CMX. The model addresses a fundamental measurement problem: traditional
marketing funnels do not capture the non-linear, multi-directional nature of community engagement.
A community member does not simply "convert" and exit; they orbit at varying levels of engagement
over time, sometimes moving closer, sometimes drifting away, and occasionally pulling others in.
The model was designed to provide community professionals with a measurement language that matches
how communities actually work.

## Core Model

### The Orbital Metaphor

Imagine the community's value proposition as a sun. Members orbit at different distances:

**Orbit 1 (Inner orbit):** Core contributors, community leaders, top advocates. These members
create value for others, moderate discussions, produce content, and recruit new members. They have
the strongest gravitational pull.

**Orbit 2 (Active orbit):** Regular participants who engage consistently. They ask and answer
questions, attend events, and are known by name. They contribute value but do not lead.

**Orbit 3 (Outer orbit):** Occasional participants. They visit periodically, consume content,
and engage when a topic resonates. They represent the growth frontier.

**Orbit 4 (Peripheral orbit):** Aware but rarely engaged. They follow social channels, receive
newsletters, or have an account but seldom participate. They are the largest group numerically.

### The Four Dimensions

**1. Gravity**
The community's ability to attract and retain members. Gravity is a function of the value
proposition's strength and the community's visibility.

Metrics:
- New member join rate
- Member retention rate (30, 60, 90 day)
- Referral rate (existing members bringing new ones)
- Inbound inquiry volume
- Time from discovery to first engagement

High gravity: New members join consistently and engage quickly. Existing members stay.
Low gravity: Stagnant growth, high early dropout, few referrals.

**2. Reach**
The breadth of the community's influence beyond its active membership. Reach measures how far the
community's content, ideas, and reputation extend.

Metrics:
- Content views and shares beyond the community
- Social media mentions of the community
- Speaking invitations and press coverage
- Audience overlap with adjacent communities
- Search visibility for community-related terms

High reach: The community is known and referenced beyond its membership.
Low reach: The community is invisible outside its active participants.

**3. Impact**
The depth and quality of individual member contributions. Impact measures what members create and
how much value their contributions generate.

Metrics:
- Content creation volume and quality
- Questions answered and problems solved
- Events organized or facilitated by members
- Product feedback submitted and implemented
- Other members helped or mentored
- Projects initiated within the community

High impact: Members create substantial value for each other and for the organization.
Low impact: Participation is shallow -- likes, brief comments, passive consumption.

**4. Love**
The emotional attachment and advocacy intensity of members. Love measures how deeply members care
about the community and its success.

Metrics:
- Net Promoter Score for the community
- Qualitative sentiment in member feedback
- Voluntary advocacy (recommending the community unprompted)
- Willingness to contribute without incentive
- Response to community challenges or threats
- Self-identification as a community member

High love: Members are passionate advocates who voluntarily invest time and energy.
Low love: Members participate transactionally, with no emotional attachment.

### Member Orbit Score

Each member can be scored across the four dimensions to determine their orbit level:

| Dimension | Orbit 1 (Core) | Orbit 2 (Active) | Orbit 3 (Occasional) | Orbit 4 (Peripheral) |
|-----------|---------------|-------------------|---------------------|---------------------|
| Engagement freq | Daily/weekly | Weekly/biweekly | Monthly | Quarterly or less |
| Content creation | Creates original content | Responds and discusses | Occasional comment | Consumes only |
| Influence | Pulls others in | Engages with newcomers | Self-focused | No interaction |
| Attachment | Strong advocate | Regular participant | Interested | Aware |

## Application Steps

### Step 1: Define Your Orbits
Establish specific behavioral criteria for each orbit level. These must be measurable and
consistent. Do not rely on subjective assessment of "how engaged" someone seems.

### Step 2: Instrument the Four Dimensions
Set up measurement for Gravity, Reach, Impact, and Love. Each dimension requires different data
sources: platform analytics for Gravity and Impact, social tools for Reach, surveys and sentiment
analysis for Love.

### Step 3: Score Your Current Membership
Calculate each member's orbit level based on their behavior. Visualize the distribution: what
percentage is in each orbit? Healthy communities have a pyramid shape (many in Orbit 4, fewer
in each inner orbit). Inverted pyramids (more core than peripheral) may indicate an echo chamber.

### Step 4: Identify Movement Patterns
Track how members move between orbits over time. Are members migrating inward (deepening
engagement) or outward (disengaging)? Net inward movement indicates a healthy community.

### Step 5: Diagnose Dimensional Weaknesses
Assess each dimension independently:
- Low Gravity: Acquisition or retention problem. The community is not attracting or keeping people.
- Low Reach: Visibility problem. The community's value is not reaching potential members.
- Low Impact: Contribution problem. Members are not creating enough value for each other.
- Low Love: Experience or value problem. Members are not forming emotional attachment.

### Step 6: Design Interventions by Dimension
Address the weakest dimension:
- Gravity: Improve onboarding, create welcome programs, increase discoverability.
- Reach: Encourage content sharing, partner with adjacent communities, create shareable content.
- Impact: Create contribution opportunities, recognize contributors, lower barriers to contribution.
- Love: Deepen relationships, create exclusive experiences, listen and respond to feedback.

### Step 7: Monitor Orbit Migration
Track the rate at which members move from outer orbits to inner orbits. This "orbit velocity" is
the leading indicator of community health. Accelerating orbit velocity indicates a thriving
community.

## Key Distinctions

- **Orbits are not a funnel.** Members do not progress linearly from Orbit 4 to Orbit 1. They
  may enter at any level and move in any direction. The model is dynamic, not sequential.
- **Not everyone should be in Orbit 1.** A healthy community has members at all levels. Orbit 4
  members still receive value (content consumption, awareness). The goal is not to push everyone
  to the center but to ensure each orbit is healthy.
- **The four dimensions are independent.** A community can have high Gravity but low Impact
  (attracts many members who do not contribute) or high Love but low Reach (passionate members
  but unknown outside the group).
- **Orbit is not status.** Orbit level describes engagement behavior, not member worth. An
  Orbit 4 member is not less valuable as a person; they simply interact differently.
- **The model applies to any community type.** Customer communities, developer communities,
  professional communities, and internal communities all exhibit orbital dynamics.

## Pitfalls

1. **Measuring only Gravity.** Growth (new members) is the most visible metric. Without Impact
   and Love, growth produces a hollow community with many members and little value.
2. **Ignoring Orbit 3 and 4.** Peripheral members are easy to forget. But they represent the
   growth frontier and often include future core members.
3. **Forced orbit migration.** Pressuring members to engage more often creates resentment, not
   attachment. Migration should be motivated by value, not obligation.
4. **Treating all dimensions equally.** At different stages, different dimensions matter more.
   Early communities need Gravity. Mature communities need Impact and Love.
5. **Static measurement.** A quarterly snapshot misses the dynamic movement that reveals community
   health. Track orbit migration over time, not just current position.
6. **Ignoring outward migration.** Members moving from Orbit 1 to Orbit 3 is a warning signal.
   Core member disengagement is often the leading indicator of community decline.
7. **Conflating platform metrics with community health.** Post count and DAU are platform metrics.
   Orbit dimensions measure community health. They correlate but are not identical.
