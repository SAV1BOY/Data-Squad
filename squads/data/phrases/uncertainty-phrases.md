# Uncertainty Phrases

## Purpose

Standardized phrases for communicating what we do not know, what we partially know, and how confident we are. Honest uncertainty language builds more trust than false precision.

## Expressing Confidence Levels

- "With 95% confidence, the true value falls between [lower bound] and [upper bound]."
- "We are [X]% confident in this finding, based on [method and sample]."
- "The margin of error on this estimate is plus or minus [X]%."
- "This result is statistically significant at p<[threshold]."
- "The confidence interval is wide, indicating substantial uncertainty in the exact magnitude."

## Acknowledging Incomplete Data

- "We do not yet have sufficient data to draw a conclusion on [topic]."
- "This analysis covers [X]% of the relevant population. The remaining [Y]% is not yet instrumented."
- "Data for [segment/period] is incomplete due to [specific reason]."
- "The sample size of [n] is below the threshold needed for reliable conclusions."
- "We are working with [X] weeks of data. A more reliable estimate requires [Y] weeks."

## Stating Hypotheses

- "Our hypothesis is that [proposed explanation]. We plan to validate this by [method] by [date]."
- "We believe [direction] is likely, but we have not yet confirmed it quantitatively."
- "Based on the available evidence, the most probable explanation is [explanation], though alternatives exist."
- "This is our best current estimate. It may change as more data becomes available."
- "We are treating this as a working assumption, not a confirmed finding."

## Expressing Directional Confidence

- "The data leans toward [direction], but the evidence is not yet conclusive."
- "Directionally, [finding]. However, we need [X] more [data/time/observations] to confirm."
- "The trend appears [positive/negative], though the magnitude is uncertain."
- "Early indicators suggest [direction], but these signals have historically been [reliable/noisy]."
- "We have a low-confidence signal that [finding]. Worth monitoring but not acting on yet."

## Communicating What We Cannot Know

- "This question cannot be answered with the data we currently collect."
- "Attribution in this case is inherently uncertain because [multiple factors/lack of controlled experiment]."
- "We can measure the correlation, but establishing causation would require [controlled experiment/different methodology]."
- "There is an irreducible uncertainty in this estimate due to [external factor/data limitation]."
- "We can narrow the range but cannot pinpoint the exact value with current methods."

## Flagging Assumptions

- "This conclusion rests on the assumption that [stated assumption]. If that assumption is wrong, [consequence]."
- "We assumed [assumption] because [rationale]. This should be validated before scaling the recommendation."
- "The projection assumes [growth rate / stable conditions / no external shocks]. Actual results may vary."
- "Key assumptions: [list]. The finding is most sensitive to changes in [specific assumption]."

## Updating Beliefs

- "New data has changed our assessment. Previously we believed [old belief]. The updated evidence suggests [new belief]."
- "We are revising our estimate from [old value] to [new value] based on [new evidence]."
- "This finding contradicts our prior assumption. We are re-evaluating [specific area]."
- "Our confidence in [finding] has [increased/decreased] based on [new information]."
